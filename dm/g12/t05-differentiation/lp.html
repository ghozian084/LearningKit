Lesson Plan: Differentiation (Direct Instruction)

School: SMA Semesta 2026/2025 | Grade: 12 (Pure Math 2 & 3)

Time Allocation: 6 Meetings (2 x 40 minutes = 80 mins per meeting)

📌 Topic: 4. Differentiation

Reference: Cambridge Mathematics Pure Math 2-3 A Level

Method: Traditional Teaching / Lecturing

Approach: Deep Learning & Realistic Mathematics Education

Character Focus: Self-Regulated, Creativity, Critical Thinking

Differentiation: Extension, Scaffolding, EAL, SEN

🎯 Objectives

  • 2.4.1 / 3.4.1: Differentiate products and quotients, including those involving exponential, logarithmic, and trigonometric functions.
  • 2.4.3 / 3.4.2: Differentiate exponential and logarithmic functions, including those requiring the chain rule.
  • 2.4.5 / 3.4.3: Differentiate trigonometric functions, including those involving inverse trigonometric functions.
  • 3.4.4 & 3.4.5: Use implicit differentiation and parametric differentiation to find derivatives.
WALT — We Are Learning To...

Learning Objective:

Master advanced differentiation techniques (Product, Quotient, Chain, Implicit, and Parametric rules) through direct instruction and rigorous practice to find the rate of change for complex and non-linear functions.


Bloom's Level:

Understand, Apply & Analyse (Levels 2–4)

WILT — What I'm Looking For...

Success Criteria (self-assessable):

  1. I can recite and apply the formulas for the Product Rule and Quotient Rule.
  2. I know the standard derivatives of $e^x$, $\ln x$, $\sin x$, $\cos x$, $\tan x$ and $\tan^{-1} x$.
  3. I can correctly apply the Chain Rule when differentiating composite functions.
  4. I can use implicit differentiation to find $\frac{dy}{dx}$ when $y$ cannot be isolated.
  5. I can find the gradient of a parametric curve by calculating $\frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$.
🖥️ Board 1 (projector slides) follows this plan meeting by meeting in the gradual-release order Lecture → I Do (teacher models) → We Do (guided) → You Do (independent). In the worked examples each step is revealed one at a time (Space / → / clicker), the "We Do" answers stay hidden until the class has shown their mini-whiteboards, and the "You Do" slides have a countdown timer.

📅 Meeting 1: The Product & Quotient Rules (Subtopics 4.1 & 4.2)

Phase 1 | Concept Introduction[15 mins]
Direct Instruction
(Teacher Lecture)

Lecture & Hook (15 min):

  • Meaningful Context: "A rectangular metal plate is being heated. Its length $L$ and width $W$ expand over time. The Area is $A = L \times W$."
  • Explain that the derivative of a product is NOT simply the product of their derivatives.
  • Write the Product Rule explicitly on the board: $\frac{dy}{dx} = u \frac{dv}{dx} + v \frac{du}{dx}$.
  • Write the Quotient Rule explicitly on the board: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{v \frac{du}{dx} - u \frac{dv}{dx}}{v^2}$.
Phase 2 | Teacher Modeling (Examples)[25 mins]
Step-by-Step Modeling

Example 1: Product Rule (10 min):

  • Teacher models finding $\frac{dy}{dx}$ for $y = x^2(2x+1)^3$.
  • Mindful: Teacher demonstrates explicitly writing out $u =, v =, u' =, v'$ in a $2 \times 2$ grid before plugging into the formula.

Example 2: Quotient Rule (15 min):

  • Teacher models finding $\frac{dy}{dx}$ for $y = \frac{3x}{x^2+1}$.
  • Joyful: Teach the chant: "Low d-High minus High d-Low, draw a line and square below!"
Phase 3 & 4 | Guided & Independent Practice[40 mins]
Student Application

Guided Practice (10 min):

  • Teacher writes one product and one quotient question on the board. Students solve it on mini-whiteboards and hold them up. Teacher corrects immediately.

Independent Exercise (30 min):

  • Students complete Worksheet 1, practicing strict application of the formulas.
  • Self-Regulated: Students verify their answers against an answer key provided at the front desk.

📅 Meeting 2: Exponentials and Logarithms (Subtopics 4.3 & 4.4)

Phase 1 | Concept Introduction[15 mins]
Direct Instruction
(Teacher Lecture)

Lecture & Hook (15 min):

  • Meaningful Context: "Population growth of bacteria is modeled by $P = e^t$. What is the rate of growth? It is exactly itself!"
  • Teacher states the standard derivatives:
    1) If $y = e^x$, then $y' = e^x$.
    2) If $y = \ln x$, then $y' = \frac{1}{x}$.
Phase 2 | Teacher Modeling (Chain Rule Integration)[25 mins]
Step-by-Step Modeling

Example 1: Chain Rule with Exponentials (15 min):

  • Teacher explains the generalized rules on the board: $y = e^{f(x)} \implies y' = f'(x)e^{f(x)}$.
  • Model solving $y = e^{3x^2 - 4x}$.

Example 2: Chain Rule with Logarithms (10 min):

  • Teacher explains $y = \ln(f(x)) \implies y' = \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}$.
  • Model solving $y = \ln(x^3 + 2x)$.
Phase 3 & 4 | Guided & Independent Practice[40 mins]
Student Application

Guided Practice (15 min):

  • Teacher models combining rules: Differentiating $y = x^2 e^{3x}$ using the Product Rule. Students follow along step-by-step.

Independent Exercise (25 min):

  • Worksheet 2: Drill exercises on exponential and logarithmic differentiation, combining them with product/quotient rules.

📅 Meeting 3: Trigonometric Functions (Subtopic 4.5)

Phase 1 | Concept Introduction[15 mins]
Direct Instruction
(Teacher Lecture)

Lecture & Hook (15 min):

  • Meaningful Context: "A pendulum swings back and forth, modeling a sine wave. Its velocity is the derivative of its position."
  • Teacher explicitly lists the 6 standard trigonometric derivatives on the board:
    $\sin x \rightarrow \cos x$ | $\cos x \rightarrow -\sin x$ | $\tan x \rightarrow \sec^2 x$
    $\sec x \rightarrow \sec x\tan x$ | $\operatorname{cosec} x \rightarrow -\operatorname{cosec} x\cot x$ | $\cot x \rightarrow -\operatorname{cosec}^2 x$  (the last three follow from the Quotient Rule)
    and the inverse function (P3): $\tan^{-1} x \rightarrow \frac{1}{1 + x^2}$.
  • Mindful: These results are only true when $x$ is in radians.
Phase 2 | Teacher Modeling (Chain Rule Applications)[25 mins]
Step-by-Step Modeling

Example 1: Linear Inner Functions (10 min):

  • Teacher demonstrates the Chain Rule application: $\frac{d}{dx}(\sin(ax+b)) = a\cos(ax+b)$.

Example 2: Powers of Trig Functions (15 min):

  • Teacher models how to rewrite $\sin^3(x)$ as $(\sin x)^3$ to apply the power chain rule: $3(\sin x)^2(\cos x) = 3\sin^2x\cos x$.
  • Critical Thinking: Ensure students see the difference between $\sin(x^2)$ and $\sin^2(x)$.
Phase 3 & 4 | Guided & Independent Practice[40 mins]
Student Application

Guided Practice (10 min):

  • Students solve $y = e^x \cos x$ on whiteboards using the Product Rule.

Independent Exercise (30 min):

  • Students complete Worksheet 3, focusing on fluency and accuracy with trigonometric derivatives.

📅 Meeting 4: Implicit Differentiation (Subtopic 4.6)

Phase 1 | Concept Introduction[15 mins]
Direct Instruction
(Teacher Lecture)

Lecture & Hook (15 min):

  • Meaningful Context: "The equation of a perfect circle is $x^2 + y^2 = 25$. We cannot isolate $y$ easily without a $\pm$ square root. We need a way to differentiate it implicitly, exactly as it is written."
  • State the rule: When differentiating a $y$ term with respect to $x$, differentiate it normally, but multiply it by $\frac{dy}{dx}$ (due to the Chain Rule).
Phase 2 | Teacher Modeling (Solving for dy/dx)[30 mins]
Step-by-Step Modeling

Example 1: Basic Implicit (10 min):

  • Teacher models finding $\frac{dy}{dx}$ for $x^2 + y^2 = 25$.
  • Shows the algebraic steps to isolate $\frac{dy}{dx}$ on one side.

Example 2: The Implicit Product Trap (20 min):

  • Teacher models differentiating $xy$ or $x^2y^3$.
  • Mindful: Explicitly demonstrate the Product Rule applied here: $u=x$, $v=y \implies 1(y) + x(1\frac{dy}{dx})$. Warn students this is the #1 mistake in A-Level exams!
Phase 3 & 4 | Guided & Independent Practice[35 mins]
Student Application

Independent Exercise (35 min):

  • Students complete Worksheet 4. They must differentiate implicit equations, collect all $\frac{dy}{dx}$ terms on one side, factorise it out, and solve.
  • Scaffold: Remind students that the derivative of an isolated constant number is ZERO.

📅 Meeting 5: Parametric Differentiation (Subtopic 4.7)

Phase 1 | Concept Introduction[15 mins]
Direct Instruction
(Teacher Lecture)

Lecture & Hook (15 min):

  • Meaningful Context: "In projectile motion, we track horizontal position ($x$) and vertical position ($y$) based on a third variable, TIME ($t$)."
  • Introduce the Parametric formula derived from the Chain Rule: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$ (or with parameter $\theta$).
Phase 2 | Teacher Modeling (Tangent Lines)[25 mins]
Step-by-Step Modeling

Example 1: Finding the Gradient (10 min):

  • Given $x = 2t$, $y = t^3 - t$. Teacher models finding $\frac{dx}{dt}$ and $\frac{dy}{dt}$ separately, then dividing them to find the gradient function $\frac{dy}{dx}$.

Example 2: Finding Tangent Equations (15 min):

  • Teacher models evaluating the gradient at $t=2$.
  • Use the gradient $m$ and the $(x, y)$ coordinates at $t=2$ to construct the tangent line equation using $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.
Phase 3 & 4 | Guided & Independent Practice[40 mins]
Student Application

Independent Exercise (40 min):

  • Students complete Worksheet 5. They calculate gradients at specific values of $t$ or $\theta$, and find the equations of tangent and normal lines.

📅 Meeting 6: Chapter Review & Summative Evaluation

Phase 1 | Teacher-Led Review[20 mins]
Concept Summary

Review of All Rules (20 min):

  • Teacher displays a summary chart of Product, Quotient, Chain, Implicit, and Parametric rules.
  • Teacher writes 5 different expressions on the board. Calls on students to identify which rule must be used for each one without actually solving it.
Phase 2 | Summative Evaluation[60 mins]
Summative Test

Chapter 4 Evaluation (55 min):

  • Distribute the Chapter Test covering products, quotients, exp/log/trig derivatives, and implicit/parametric gradients.
  • Self-Regulated: Students must manage their time and rely on their own memory of the formulas.

Closure (5 min):

  • Collect tests and briefly discuss the most challenging question on the paper.

🔑 Teacher Key — Worked Examples on the Board

  • M1: $y = x^2(2x+1)^3 \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = 6x^2(2x+1)^2 + 2x(2x+1)^3 = 2x(2x+1)^2(5x+1)$ · $y = \frac{3x}{x^2+1} \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{3(1 - x^2)}{(x^2+1)^2}$ · We Do: $y = x(3x-1)^4 \Rightarrow (3x-1)^3(15x-1)$; $y = \frac{2x-1}{x+3} \Rightarrow \frac{7}{(x+3)^2}$
  • M2: $y = e^{3x^2-4x} \Rightarrow (6x-4)e^{3x^2-4x}$ · $y = \ln(x^3+2x) \Rightarrow \frac{3x^2+2}{x^3+2x}$ · $y = x^2e^{3x} \Rightarrow xe^{3x}(3x+2)$
  • M3: $\sin(3x+1) \to 3\cos(3x+1)$; $\cos(2x - \frac{\pi}{4}) \to -2\sin(2x - \frac{\pi}{4})$; $\tan 5x \to 5\sec^2 5x$ · $\sin^3 x \to 3\sin^2 x\cos x$; $\sin(x^2) \to 2x\cos(x^2)$; $\sin^2 x \to 2\sin x\cos x = \sin 2x$ · $e^x\cos x \to e^x(\cos x - \sin x)$
  • M4: $x^2 + y^2 = 25 \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{x}{y}$, at $(3, 4)$: $-\frac34$ · $x^2 + 3xy + y^2 = 11 \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{2x + 3y}{3x + 2y}$, at $(1, 2)$: $-\frac87$
  • M5: $x = 2t$, $y = t^3 - t \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{3t^2 - 1}{2}$; at $t = 2$: point $(4, 6)$, $m = \frac{11}{2}$, tangent $2y = 11x - 32$, normal $2x + 11y = 74$
🌟 Differentiation Strategies
Profile Strategies & Actions in Class
Extension (High Achievers) Require finding the second derivative $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ for parametric equations (which requires the chain rule $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right) \times \frac{dt}{dx}$). Challenge them with finding stationary points on implicit curves where $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$.
Scaffolding (Middle/Low) Mandate the use of a rigid grid layout for Product and Quotient rules (draw a $2 \times 2$ box: $u$, $v$, $u'$, $v'$). Provide the trig derivative circle ($\sin \rightarrow \cos \rightarrow -\sin \rightarrow -\cos$) as a visual aid on the board during independent practice.
EAL (English Additional Language) Provide a glossary: Derivative (Turunan), Tangent (Garis singgung), Gradient (Gradien), Implicit (Implisit). Ensure they understand the phrase "differentiate with respect to $x$".
SEN (Special Educational Needs) Use color-coding strictly on the board during modeling. In implicit differentiation, write all $y$ terms in red marker and all $x$ terms in blue marker so they remember to attach $\frac{dy}{dx}$ to the red terms only.
