Meeting 4 · Stimulation Two Friends, Two Exams

Hook 📝

Noa and Sunja take exams in different rooms. Does Noa\'s score change Sunja\'s? No — independent events.

Also: flip a coin twice; draw a candy and put it back.

Problem Statement

$P(\text{Noa passes}) = \frac34$, $P(\text{Sunja passes}) = \frac56$. $P(\text{both})$?

Why NOT $\frac34 + \frac56$? (It is more than 1!)

Both pass?

Meeting 4 · Data Spin, Then Shade

Hands-on 4 🎡 (or Interactive Media 4)

Spin both spinners 24 times; count "both pass". Shade the $4 \times 6$ area model: 15 of 24 cells.

Two coins: HH, HT, TH, TT → $P(HH) = \frac14 = \frac12 \times \frac12$

Processing

Independent: $P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \times P(B)$   AND = ×

$\frac34 \times \frac56 = \frac58$  ·  neither: $\frac14 \times \frac16 = \frac{1}{24}$

Meeting 5 · Stimulation Futsal or Robotics?

Hook ⚽

Futsal and Robotics both meet Tuesday at 3:00 PM — you can\'t do both: mutually exclusive.

Smartphone AND glasses? Even AND less than 4 on a die? (2 fits both!)

Problem Statement

When events CANNOT happen together, how do we find $P(A \text{ or } B)$?

Together or not?

Meeting 5 · Data Sort, Test, Combine

Hands-on 5 🃏 (or Interactive Media 5)

Sort 16 cards: can / cannot happen together. Test die events on the Venn mat.

Mutually exclusive: $P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B)$   OR = +

Processing

At least one passes: $1 - \frac{1}{24} = \frac{23}{24}$

Exactly one: $\frac34 \cdot \frac16 + \frac14 \cdot \frac56 = \frac{3}{24} + \frac{5}{24} = \frac13$

Simone 0.1, Rami 0.2: exactly one $= 0.08 + 0.18 = 0.26$

Meeting 6 · Stimulation Spin-the-Wheel Food Stall

Synthesis 🍪🥤

Square spinner (1–4) + pentagon spinner (1–5): 20 outcomes.

🍪 A: total 5 → snack · 🥤 B: difference 1 → drink.

Prove it

$P(A) = \frac{4}{20}$, $P(B) = \frac{7}{20}$, but $P(A \text{ or } B) = \frac{9}{20} \ne \frac{11}{20}$. Why?

(2, 3) and (3, 2) win BOTH → not mutually exclusive.
