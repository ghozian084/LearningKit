Lesson Plan: Fractions, Percentages & Standard Form

School: SMP Semesta 2026/2025 | Grade: 9

Time Allocation: 4 Meetings (2 x 40 minutes = 80 mins per meeting)

📌 Topic: 5. Fractions, percentages and standard form

Reference: Cambridge IGCSE® Math Core & Extended

Method: Project Based Learning (PjBL)

Approach: Hands-on Activity & Deep Learning

Character Focus: Health, Self-Regulated, Collaboration, Creativity, Critical Thinking

Differentiation: Extension, Scaffolding, EAL, SEN

🎯 Objectives

  • C1.4, E1.4: Use the language and notation of proper/improper fractions and mixed numbers; Recognise equivalence and convert between forms.
  • C1.6, E1.6: Use the four operations for calculations with fractions.
  • C1.13, E1.13: Calculate a given percentage of a quantity; Express one quantity as a percentage of another; Calculate percentage increase or decrease.
  • C1.8, E1.8: Use the standard form $A \times 10^n$; Convert numbers into and out of standard form; Calculate with values in standard form.
WALT — We Are Learning To...

Learning Objective:

Apply fractions, percentages, and standard form to analyze global and personal health data, scaling nutritional recipes, calculating caloric changes, and measuring microscopic/macroscopic statistics.


Bloom's Level:

Apply, Analyse & Create (Levels 3–6)

WILT — What I'm Looking For...

Success Criteria (self-assessable):

  1. I can convert fluently between mixed numbers and improper fractions.
  2. I can add, subtract, multiply, and divide fractions to scale recipes.
  3. I can calculate percentage increases/decreases in health metrics (e.g., heart rate, calories).
  4. I can convert microscopic sizes (viruses) and large populations into standard form ($A \times 10^n$).
  5. I can collaborate to build a "Global Nutrition & Micro-Health Portfolio".
🏥 PjBL Theme: "The Global Nutrition & Micro-Health Portfolio"
Students act as health and nutrition analysts. Throughout these 4 meetings, they will apply fraction operations to scale healthy traditional recipes, use percentages to analyze caloric and nutritional intake changes, and apply standard form to understand microscopic health threats (viruses/bacteria) vs massive global health statistics.

📅 Meeting 1: Fractions Recap & Project Launch (Subtopics 5.1 & 5.2)

PjBL Phase 1 | Essential Question & Hook[20 mins]
Teacher-led & Class Discussion

Hook — The Recipe Dilemma (10 min):

  • Health Context: Show a healthy recipe for *Klepon*. "This serves 4 people and needs $1 \frac{1}{2}$ cups of flour. How do we make it for the whole class of 30?"

Project Launch (10 min):

  • Introduce the "Nutrition & Health Portfolio" project. Assign groups (See Appendix).
PjBL Phase 2 | Operations on Fractions[60 mins]
Hands-On Activity & Practice

Rapid Concept Review (20 min):

  • Joyful: Use physical plastic fraction circles to demonstrate why we need a Common Denominator for Addition/Subtraction.
  • Demonstrate Paper Folding for Multiplication (Top $\times$ Top, Bottom $\times$ Bottom).
  • Division: "Keep, Change, Flip." Remind students to convert mixed numbers to improper fractions first!

Elaborate: Scaling Recipes (40 min):

  • Groups select a traditional healthy Indonesian recipe. They must use fraction operations to scale it up for a school festival (50 people) and scale it down for exactly 3 people. This forms Page 1 of their Portfolio.
  • Scaffold: Provide SEN students with physical fraction bars to assist with calculations.

📅 Meeting 2: Percentages & Standard Form (Subtopics 5.3 & 5.4)

PjBL Phase 3 | Percentages in Health Data[40 mins]
Direct Instruction & Discovery

Discovery: The Multiplier Method (20 min):

  • Meaningful: "A sports drink claims '20% less sugar!' If the original had 30g, how much is in the new one?"
  • Teach students to use multipliers ($0.80 \times 30g$) instead of subtracting.
  • Percentage change: $\frac{\text{Difference}}{\text{Original}} \times 100\%$.

Elaborate (20 min):

  • Groups analyze a "Before & After" health scenario (e.g., a person's resting heart rate drops from 85 bpm to 68 bpm after jogging for a month). Calculate percentage change for the Portfolio.
PjBL Phase 3 | The Micro and Macro (Standard Form)[40 mins]
Visual Hook & Practice

Concept: Standard Form (20 min):

  • Critical Thinking: "The Covid-19 virus is $0.00000012$ m wide. Indonesia's population is $275,000,000$. How do scientists write these without making a mistake counting zeros?"
  • Teach $A \times 10^n$, where $1 \le A < 10$. Show how positive $n$ jumps right, negative $n$ jumps left.
  • Multiplying standard form: $(2 \times 10^3) \times (4 \times 10^5) = 8 \times 10^8$. Use Index Laws!

Elaborate (20 min):

  • Worksheet on Standard Form calculations to prepare for the final project assembly.

📅 Meeting 3: Project Assembly & Synthesis

PjBL Phase 4 | Artifact Generation[80 mins]
Collaborative Project Work

Project Task: The Micro-Health Factsheet (40 min):

  • Groups research (or are provided) 5 massive global health statistics and 5 microscopic biological measurements relevant to their assigned group theme.
  • They must convert all data into Standard Form and create a visual ranking chart for their Portfolio.

Finalizing the Health Portfolio (40 min):

  • Groups assemble their 3 pages: 1) The Scaled Healthy Recipe (Fractions), 2) The Health Improvement Analysis (Percentages), 3) The Micro/Macro Data Chart (Standard Form).
  • Self-Regulated: Groups double-check all calculations against the success criteria rubric before submission.
  • Scaffold: EAL students practice translating "Scientific Notation" as a synonym for Standard Form.

📅 Meeting 4: Showcase & Summative Evaluation

PjBL Phase 5 | Presenting Solutions[20 mins]
Student-led Gallery Walk

Health Portfolio Gallery Walk (20 min):

  • Portfolios are displayed. Students evaluate peers' work checking for accurate fraction scaling and correct standard form formatting.
  • Teacher assesses communication and mathematical accuracy artifacts.
PjBL Phase 6 | Evaluating the Experience[60 mins]
Summative Test

Chapter 5 Evaluation (55 min):

  • Remove visual aids. Distribute the Chapter 5 Test covering mixed number arithmetic, percentage changes, and standard form operations.

Closure & Reflection (5 min):

  • Self-Reflection: "How does writing numbers in standard form prevent dangerous medical/scientific errors?"
🌟 Differentiation Strategies
Profile Strategies & Actions in Class
Extension (High Achievers) Challenge with complex fraction division involving algebraic variables. Provide standard form addition/subtraction problems (which require making indices match first before adding the mantissa). Ask them to calculate compound percentage changes (e.g., population growth over 5 years).
Scaffolding (Middle/Low) Provide fraction bars/circles to physically verify arithmetic during worksheet time. Give a "Percentage Multiplier Translation Sheet" (e.g., 20% discount $\rightarrow$ Multiply by 0.8).
EAL (English Additional Language) Provide a bilingual glossary: Numerator (Pembilang), Denominator (Penyebut), Proper Fraction (Pecahan Biasa), Mixed Number (Pecahan Campuran). Emphasize visual steps over dense word problems.
SEN (Special Educational Needs) Draw visual "loops" or "frog jumps" physically on paper when converting decimals to Standard Form. Highlight the target integer ($A$) in a bright color to ensure they place the decimal correctly.

Appendix: PjBL Group Missions & Execution Guide

Each group is assigned a unique health topic. They must apply Fractions, Percentages, and Standard Form to their specific domain to build their "Global Nutrition & Micro-Health Portfolio" in Meeting 3.

🟢 Group 1: The Dietary Planners (Nutrition Focus)

  • Fractions: Scale a healthy salad recipe (using mixed numbers like $1 \frac{3}{4}$ cups of spinach) originally for 4 people up to feed 18 people.
  • Percentages: A student replaces sugary soda with water. Their daily sugar intake drops from 65g to 25g. Calculate the exact percentage decrease.
  • Standard Form: Research the size of a Vitamin C molecule and write it in Standard Form. Compare it to the global annual production of Vitamin C (in kg).

🔵 Group 2: The Epidemiologists (Disease Focus)

  • Fractions: A hospital has $\frac{5}{8}$ of its beds occupied. If they add 120 new beds, what fraction is now occupied? (Calculate new fractional capacity).
  • Percentages: A vaccination campaign reduces flu cases from 4,500 to 1,200 in one month. Calculate the percentage decrease.
  • Standard Form: Write the exact diameter of the Influenza virus ($0.0000001$ meters) and the global population ($8,100,000,000$) in Standard Form. Calculate how many viruses laid end-to-end span the equator.

🔴 Group 3: The Fitness Coaches (Exercise Focus)

  • Fractions: A runner completes $3 \frac{1}{2}$ laps of a track. Each lap is $\frac{2}{5}$ of a kilometer. Calculate the total distance run using fraction multiplication.
  • Percentages: An athlete's resting heart rate improves from 78 bpm to 62 bpm after a training camp. Express the improvement as a percentage decrease.
  • Standard Form: An average human heart beats about $35,000,000$ times a year. Write this in Standard Form. Calculate how many times it beats in an 80-year lifespan, leaving the answer in Standard Form.

🟡 Group 4: The Hydration Specialists (Water Focus)

  • Fractions: A water dispenser holds $15 \frac{1}{2}$ liters. If a student's bottle holds $\frac{3}{4}$ of a liter, how many full bottles can be filled from the dispenser?
  • Percentages: A student aims to drink 2.5 liters of water a day. By noon, they have drunk 800 ml. What percentage of their daily goal have they reached?
  • Standard Form: The human body contains approximately $3.7 \times 10^{13}$ cells, and each cell is mostly water. Calculate the total number of cells in a classroom of 30 students using Standard Form multiplication.

💡 Teacher Tips for Execution

  1. Portfolio Assembly (Meeting 3): Provide A3 colored cardstock for the groups to glue their calculations onto. This makes the Gallery Walk in Meeting 4 much more visually engaging.
  2. Fraction Manipulatives (Meeting 1): Use physical plastic fraction circles (or paper cut-outs) when reviewing addition/subtraction to ensure students visually remember why finding a Common Denominator is mandatory.
  3. The "Jump" Method (Meeting 2): When teaching Standard Form, explicitly draw the "frog jumps" under the zeros on the whiteboard to help SEN and visual learners count the index $n$ accurately.
