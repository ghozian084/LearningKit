Lesson Plan: Logarithmic and Exponential Functions

School: SMA Semesta 2026/2025 | Grade: 12

Time Allocation: 12 Meetings (2 x 40 minutes = 80 mins per meeting)

📌 Topic: 2. Logarithmic and exponential functions

Reference: Cambridge Mathematics Pure Math 2-3 A Level

Method: 5E, Problem & Project Based Learning (PjBL)

Approach: Math Modelling & Hands-on Activity

Character Focus: Self-Regulated, Collaboration, Creativity, Critical Thinking

Differentiation: Extension, Scaffolding, EAL, SEN

🎯 Objectives

  • 2.2.1 / 3.2.1: Understand the relationship between logarithms and exponentials, and use the laws of logarithms (including change of base).
  • 2.2.2 / 3.2.2: Solve equations of the form $a^x = b$. Solve equations involving exponential and logarithmic expressions.
  • 2.2.3: Transform a relationship to linear form to determine constants, e.g. $y = kx^n$ to $\lg y = \lg k + n \lg x$, or $y = ka^x$ to $\lg y = \lg k + x \lg a$.
WALT — We Are Learning To...

Learning Objective:

Understand the relationship between logarithms and exponentials, apply the laws of logarithms to solve complex equations, and transform non-linear relationships into linear forms to find unknown constants through mathematical modelling.


Bloom's Level:

Apply, Analyse & Evaluate (Levels 3–5)

WILT — What I'm Looking For...

Success Criteria (self-assessable):

  1. I can convert fluently between exponential and logarithmic forms.
  2. I can apply the product, quotient, and power laws to simplify log expressions.
  3. I can use the change of base formula to solve equations with different bases.
  4. I can solve exponential equations and inequalities by taking the logarithm of both sides.
  5. I can transform equations like $y = ka^x$ into a linear straight line $Y = mX + C$ and use a graph to determine the constants.

📅 Meeting 1: Logarithms to Base 10 (Subtopic 2.1)

E1 & E2 | Engage & Explore[35 mins]
Teacher-led & Hands-on

Hook — Richter Scale (15 min):

  • Meaningful: Display news about a 5.0 and 6.0 earthquake in Indonesia. "Is a 6.0 just slightly stronger than a 5.0? No, it's 10 times stronger! This is a Logarithmic Scale."

Explore — The Inverse Function (20 min):

  • Ask students to solve $10^x = 100$ and $10^x = 1000$. Then ask them to solve $10^x = 500$.
  • Students realize they need a mathematical tool to "undo" an exponent. Define $\log_{10}(x)$.
E3 & E4 | Explain & Elaborate[45 mins]
Direct Instruction & Practice

Direct Instruction — Slides 1-3 (20 min):

  • Define $y = \log_{10} x \iff 10^y = x$.
  • Notation: $\log_{10}$ is often written as $\lg$ or simply $\log$.
  • Model graphing $y = 10^x$ and $y = \log_{10} x$. Show they are reflections across $y = x$.

Elaborate (25 min):

  • Students complete Worksheet 1 converting back and forth between exponent and log forms.
  • Scaffold: Remind students that logs cannot take negative inputs! (Domain of log is $x > 0$).

📅 Meeting 2: Logarithms to Base $a$ (Subtopic 2.2)

E1 & E2 | Engage & Explore[30 mins]
Concept Building

Hook — Computer Binary (10 min):

  • "Computers operate on base 2. If memory doubles every generation, we use $2^x$. How do we find $x$?" We use $\log_2$!

Explore (20 min):

  • Students evaluate $\log_2 8$, $\log_3 81$, and $\log_5 125$ by mental trial and error.
E3 & E4 | Explain & Elaborate[50 mins]
Direct Instruction & Practice

Direct Instruction (20 min):

  • General definition: $y = \log_a x \iff a^y = x$.
  • Special values: $\log_a a = 1$ and $\log_a 1 = 0$.

Elaborate & Evaluate (30 min):

  • Worksheet 2: Solving simple expressions like $\log_4 x = 3$ or $\log_x 64 = 2$.
  • Exit Ticket: Evaluate $\log_7 49$.

📅 Meeting 3: The Laws of Logarithms (Subtopic 2.3)

E1 & E2 | Engage & Explore[30 mins]
Historical Context & Discovery

Hook — Napier's Bones (10 min):

  • "Before calculators, astronomers multiplied 8-digit numbers by adding their logarithms using a Slide Rule. Let's see how."

Discovery (20 min):

  • Have students calculate $\log_{10}(100) + \log_{10}(1000)$ and compare it to $\log_{10}(100 \times 1000)$.
  • Joyful: Students deduce the Product Law naturally!
E3 & E4 | Formalizing the 3 Laws[50 mins]
Direct Instruction & Practice

Direct Instruction (20 min):

  • 1. Product Law: $\log_a (xy) = \log_a x + \log_a y$
  • 2. Quotient Law: $\log_a (x/y) = \log_a x - \log_a y$
  • 3. Power Law: $\log_a (x^n) = n \log_a x$

Elaborate (30 min):

  • Worksheet 3: Compressing and expanding logarithmic expressions.
  • Scaffold: Emphasize that $\log(A+B)$ is NOT $\log A + \log B$. A common A-Level trap!

📅 Meeting 4 & 5: Solving Logarithmic Equations & Change of Base (Subtopics 2.4 & 3.2.1)

E1 & E2 | Combining Laws to Solve[40 mins]
Problem Based Learning

Hook (Meeting 4) (15 min):

  • "Solve $\log_3 x + \log_3(x-2) = 1$."
  • Groups attempt to solve. They must realize they must use the Product Law to combine the logs first before converting to exponential form!

Explore (25 min):

  • Groups solve and get a quadratic equation: $x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0$.
  • Mindful: They get $x=3$ and $x=-1$. Ask: "Can we use $x=-1$?" (No, cannot log a negative number. Reject extraneous roots!).
E3 & E4 | Change of Base Theorem[120 mins across M4/M5]
Direct Instruction

Direct Instruction (Meeting 5) (30 min):

  • What if the bases don't match? Solve $\log_2 x + \log_4 x = 3$.
  • Introduce Change of Base Formula: $\log_a b = \frac{\log_c b}{\log_c a}$.
  • Show the special case: $\log_a b = \frac{1}{\log_b a}$.

Elaborate & Evaluate (90 min over two days):

  • Worksheets 4 & 5. High-level algebraic manipulation requiring substitution (e.g., let $u = \log_2 x$) to solve hidden quadratics.

📅 Meeting 6 & 7: Exponential Equations & Inequalities (Subtopics 2.5 & 2.6)

E1 & E3 | The Power of Bringing it Down[60 mins]
Teacher-led Instruction

Direct Instruction (Meeting 6):

  • Solving $a^x = b$. (e.g., $3^x = 20$).
  • Method: Take $\log$ or $\ln$ of both sides. Use Power Law to bring $x$ down. $x \log 3 = \log 20$.
  • Solving complex forms: $2^{x+1} = 3^{2x-1}$. Requires careful expansion and factorisation of $x$.
E3 & E4 | Inequalities & Negative Logs[100 mins]
Guided Practice & Elaboration

Direct Instruction (Meeting 7):

  • Solving $0.5^x < 10$. Take logs: $x \log(0.5) < \log(10)$.
  • Critical Thinking: Since $0.5 < 1$, $\log(0.5)$ is a NEGATIVE number. When dividing by a negative, the inequality sign MUST FLIP!

Elaborate:

  • Worksheets 6 & 7. Solving exponential inequalities and hidden quadratics ($3^{2x} - 4(3^x) + 3 = 0$, let $u = 3^x$).

📅 Meeting 8: Natural Logarithms (Subtopic 2.7)

E1 & E3 | Euler's Number (e)[40 mins]
Concept Building

Hook — Continuous Compounding (10 min):

  • If an Indonesian bank offers 100% interest compounded yearly, you get 2x. If compounded monthly? Daily? Every second? It approaches a magical limit: $e \approx 2.718$.

Direct Instruction (30 min):

  • Define $e$ as the base of natural growth.
  • Define $\ln x$ as $\log_e x$. Explain that $\ln(e) = 1$ and $\ln(e^x) = x$.
  • Solve continuous decay problems (e.g., radioactive isotopes).

📅 Meetings 9-12: PjBL - Transforming to Linear Form (Subtopic 2.8)

PjBL Phase 1 & 2 | Concept & Project Launch (Meeting 9)[80 mins]
Direct Instruction &
Project Design

Concept: Linear Form (40 min):

  • Scientists collect data that follows an exponential curve $y = A b^x$. It's hard to find $A$ and $b$ from a curve.
  • Take $\log$ of both sides: $\log y = \log A + x \log b$.
  • Map this to $Y = mX + c$. So plotting $\log y$ against $x$ gives a straight line with gradient $\log b$ and intercept $\log A$!

🪝 Project Launch: "The Cooling Curve" (40 min):

  • Task: In groups, you will model the exponential cooling of hot tea (or water draining) in the lab.
  • Design: Plan your equipment (thermometers, stopwatches) and data table ($t$ vs $Temp$).
PjBL Phase 3 | Data Collection (Meeting 10)[80 mins]
Hands-On Lab Work

Lab Execution:

  • Groups boil water, pour it into a cup, and measure the temperature every 2 minutes for 40 minutes.
  • Collaboration: One reads thermometer, one manages stopwatch, one records data.
  • Calculate $(T - T_{\text{room}})$ to get the true exponential decay value $y$.
PjBL Phase 4 | Mathematical Modelling (Meeting 11)[80 mins]
Data Processing

Graphing & Analysis:

  • Groups create a new column for $\ln y$ (Natural log of temperature difference).
  • Plot $\ln y$ on the vertical axis against time $t$ on the horizontal axis using graph paper.
  • Draw a Line of Best Fit.
  • Calculate the gradient ($m$) and y-intercept ($c$).
  • Meaningful: Reverse the log process to find the original formula constants $A$ and $k$ for their specific cup of tea! $y = A e^{kt}$.
Scaffold students: Provide Excel/Desmos to check their manual gradient calculations if they get stuck.
PjBL Phase 5 & Summative | Showcase & Exam (Meeting 12)[80 mins]
Evaluation

Project Showcase (30 min):

  • Groups display their graphs and final mathematical model formulas. Brief class discussion on whose cup cooled the fastest (which $k$ value was largest) and why (insulation, surface area).

Chapter 2 Summative Evaluation (50 min):

  • Cambridge A-Level standard test covering Laws of Logs, solving log/exponential equations, and a theoretical Linear Form question.
🌟 Differentiation Strategies
Profile Strategies & Actions in Class
Extension (High Achievers) Assign complex hidden quadratic equations ($e^{2x} - 5e^x + 6 = 0$). For the Linear Form project, ask them to transform $y = k x^n$ (power model) instead of $y = kb^x$ and explain the difference in the graph ($X$-axis becomes $\log x$).
Scaffolding (Middle/Low) Provide a physical "Laws of Logs" cheat sheet to keep on their desk. For the PjBL graph, provide axes that are pre-scaled, and guide them carefully on picking two points far apart to calculate the gradient.
EAL (English Additional Language) Clarify vocabulary: Base (Basis), Exponent/Power (Pangkat), Gradient (Gradien/Kemiringan), Intercept (Titik potong).
SEN (Special Educational Needs) During the PjBL, assign clear, structured roles (e.g., Timekeeper). Use large-grid graph paper to plot the Linear Form data to reduce visual strain.
