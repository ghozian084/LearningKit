Meeting 1 · Lecture The Heated Metal Plate

Hook 🔥

A metal plate is heated: its length $L$ and width $W$ both grow. Area $A = L \times W$. How fast does $A$ grow?

Trap: is $(uv)' = u'v'$? Test $y = x \cdot x^2 = x^3$: the true answer is $3x^2$, but $u'v' = 1 \times 2x = 2x$ ❌

Product Rule

$y = uv \;\Rightarrow\; \dfrac{dy}{dx} = u\dfrac{dv}{dx} + v\dfrac{du}{dx}$

Quotient Rule

$y = \dfrac{u}{v} \;\Rightarrow\; \dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{v\dfrac{du}{dx} - u\dfrac{dv}{dx}}{v^2}$

Why not u′v′?

Meeting 1 · I Do Example 1 · Product Rule

$y = x^2(2x+1)^3$  — find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$

🧘 Mindful: always fill the $2 \times 2$ grid first.

$u = x^2$$v = (2x+1)^3$
$u' = 2x$$v' = 3(2x+1)^2 \times 2 = 6(2x+1)^2$
Rule
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = uv' + vu' = x^2 \cdot 6(2x+1)^2 + (2x+1)^3 \cdot 2x$
Factorise
common factor $2x(2x+1)^2$:  $= 2x(2x+1)^2\,[\,3x + (2x+1)\,]$
Answer
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2x(2x+1)^2(5x+1)$
Teacher Working

Meeting 1 · I Do Example 2 · Quotient Rule

$y = \dfrac{3x}{x^2+1}$  — find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$
🎵 "Low d-High minus High d-Low, draw a line and square below!"
$u = 3x$ (High)$v = x^2 + 1$ (Low)
$u' = 3$$v' = 2x$
Rule
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{(x^2+1)(3) - (3x)(2x)}{(x^2+1)^2}$
Expand
$= \dfrac{3x^2 + 3 - 6x^2}{(x^2+1)^2} = \dfrac{3 - 3x^2}{(x^2+1)^2}$
Answer
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{3(1 - x^2)}{(x^2+1)^2}$   ⚠️ order matters: $v u'$ comes first!
Teacher Working

Meeting 2 · Lecture Exponentials & Logarithms

Hook 🦠

Bacteria: $P = e^t$. The rate of growth is… exactly itself! $\frac{dP}{dt} = e^t$

Standard derivatives

$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(e^x\right) = e^x \qquad \dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\ln x\right) = \dfrac{1}{x}$

With the Chain Rule

$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(e^{f(x)}\right) = f'(x)\,e^{f(x)} \qquad \dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\ln f(x)\right) = \dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}$

🤔 Puzzle: $\frac{d}{dx}\ln(3x) = \frac{3}{3x} = \frac1x$ — the same as $\ln x$! Why? ($\ln 3x = \ln 3 + \ln x$)
Notes

Meeting 2 · I Do Example 1 · Chain Rule with $e$

$y = e^{3x^2 - 4x}$  — find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$
Step 1
The power is $f(x) = 3x^2 - 4x$, so $f'(x) = 6x - 4$.
Step 2
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = f'(x)\,e^{f(x)} = (6x - 4)\,e^{3x^2 - 4x}$
Answer
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2(3x - 2)\,e^{3x^2 - 4x}$
🧘 The power stays exactly the same — we only multiply by $f'(x)$ in front.
Teacher Working

Meeting 2 · I Do Example 2 · Chain Rule with $\ln$

$y = \ln(x^3 + 2x)$  — find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$
Step 1
Inside: $f(x) = x^3 + 2x$, so $f'(x) = 3x^2 + 2$.
Step 2
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{f'(x)}{f(x)}$
Answer
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{3x^2 + 2}{x^3 + 2x}$
❌ Common error: $\dfrac{1}{x^3 + 2x}$ — forgetting to multiply by $f'(x)$. And never "cancel" terms in a sum!
Teacher Working

Meeting 2 · We Do Follow Along · Combining Rules

$y = x^2 e^{3x}$  — Product Rule + Chain Rule

✍️ Copy each line into your book as it appears.

$u = x^2$$v = e^{3x}$
$u' = 2x$$v' = 3e^{3x}$
Rule
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = x^2 \cdot 3e^{3x} + e^{3x} \cdot 2x$
Answer
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = x e^{3x}(3x + 2)$
Your Turn: y = x³e²ˣ

Meeting 3 · Lecture Trigonometric Derivatives

🕰️ Hook: a pendulum's position is a sine wave; its velocity is the derivative of its position.

The 6 standard derivatives

$\sin x \to \cos x$$\cos x \to -\sin x$$\tan x \to \sec^2 x$
$\sec x \to \sec x\tan x$$\operatorname{cosec} x \to -\operatorname{cosec} x\cot x$$\cot x \to -\operatorname{cosec}^2 x$

(row 2 comes from the Quotient Rule)  ·  P3: $\tan^{-1} x \to \dfrac{1}{1 + x^2}$

⚠️ Radians only! These results are true only when $x$ is measured in radians.
Notes

Meeting 3 · I Do Example 1 · Linear Inner Functions

$\dfrac{d}{dx}\big(\sin(ax + b)\big) = a\cos(ax + b)$

differentiate the outside, keep the inside, multiply by the inside's derivative $a$

(i)
$y = \sin(3x + 1) \;\Rightarrow\; $ $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3\cos(3x + 1)$
(ii)
$y = \cos\left(2x - \frac{\pi}{4}\right) \;\Rightarrow\; $ $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -2\sin\left(2x - \frac{\pi}{4}\right)$
(iii)
$y = \tan 5x \;\Rightarrow\; $ $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 5\sec^2 5x$
Teacher Working

Meeting 3 · I Do Example 2 · Powers of Trig Functions

$y = \sin^3 x$
Rewrite
$\sin^3 x = (\sin x)^3$  — a power of a function
Chain
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3(\sin x)^2 \times \cos x = $ $3\sin^2 x\cos x$

🧠 Critical thinking: spot the difference

$y = \sin(x^2)$ — angle squared$y = \sin^2 x = (\sin x)^2$ — sine squared
inside $x^2$: $\;\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2x\cos(x^2)$power: $\;\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2\sin x\cos x = \sin 2x$
Teacher Working

Meeting 3 · We Do Mini-Whiteboards

$y = e^x\cos x$  — Product Rule

✍️ Grid → rule → factorise. Boards up on 3 – 2 – 1!

$u = e^x,\ u' = e^x$$v = \cos x,\ v' = -\sin x$
Answer
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = e^x(-\sin x) + \cos x \cdot e^x = $ $e^x(\cos x - \sin x)$
💡 Extension: where is the gradient zero for $0 \le x \le 2\pi$? ($\tan x = 1$: $x = \frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{5\pi}{4}$)

Meeting 4 · Lecture Implicit Differentiation

Hook ⭕

Circle: $x^2 + y^2 = 25$. Isolating $y$ gives $y = \pm\sqrt{25 - x^2}$ — messy! Let's differentiate it exactly as it is written.

The rule (Chain Rule)

$\dfrac{d}{dx}\left({\color{#c53030}{y^n}}\right) = {\color{#c53030}{ny^{n-1}\dfrac{dy}{dx}}}$

differentiate a $y$-term normally, then multiply by $\frac{dy}{dx}$

e.g.
$\dfrac{d}{dx}(''' + B % 'x^2' + r''') = ''' + B % '2x' + r''' \qquad \dfrac{d}{dx}(''' + R % 'y^2' + r''') = ''' + R % r'2y\dfrac{dy}{dx}' + r''' \qquad \dfrac{d}{dx}(''' + R % 'y^3' + r''') = ''' + R % r'3y^2\dfrac{dy}{dx}' + r''' \qquad \dfrac{d}{dx}(25) = 0$''
🎨 Colour code: $x$-terms blue, $y$-terms red — only the red terms get $\frac{dy}{dx}$.
Notes

Meeting 4 · I Do Example 1 · The Circle

$x^2 + y^2 = 25$  — find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$, then the gradient at $(3, 4)$
Differentiate
$''' + B % '2x' + ' + ' + R % r'2y\dfrac{dy}{dx}' + r''' = 0$
Isolate
$2y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -2x$
Solve
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{x}{y}$
At (3, 4)
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac34$
✅ Check: the radius to $(3, 4)$ has gradient $\frac43$; the tangent is perpendicular, so $-\frac34$. ✔
Teacher Working

Meeting 4 · I Do Example 2 · The Implicit Product Trap

⚠️ The #1 mistake in A-Level exams: $xy$ is a product!
$xy$
$u = x,\ v = y$:   $\dfrac{d}{dx}(xy) = 1 \cdot y + x \cdot 1\dfrac{dy}{dx} = $ $y + x\dfrac{dy}{dx}$
$x^2y^3$
$u = x^2,\ v = y^3$:   $\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^2y^3) = 2x \cdot y^3 + x^2 \cdot 3y^2\dfrac{dy}{dx}$
Simplify
$2xy^3 + 3x^2y^2\dfrac{dy}{dx}$

❌ Wrong: $\frac{d}{dx}(xy) = \frac{dy}{dx}$  or  $1 \cdot \frac{dy}{dx}$ — this forgets one of the two terms.

Teacher Working

Meeting 4 · I Do Example 2 (continued) · Solve for dy/dx

$x^2 + 3xy + y^2 = 11$  — gradient at $(1, 2)$
Check
$(1, 2)$ is on the curve: $1 + 6 + 4 = 11$ ✓
Differentiate
$2x + \left(3y + 3x\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right) + 2y\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0$
Collect
$(3x + 2y)\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -(2x + 3y)$
Solve
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{2x + 3y}{3x + 2y}$
At (1, 2)
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = -\dfrac{2 + 6}{3 + 4} = -\dfrac87$

Meeting 5 · Lecture Parametric Differentiation

Hook ⚽

A ball: $x = 20t$, $\;y = 15t - 5t^2$ — both depend on time $t$.

Parametric gradient

$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{dy}{dt} \times \dfrac{dt}{dx} = \dfrac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$

(the same with parameter $\theta$)

Ball
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{15 - 10t}{20}$; the top of the flight is where $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 0$: $\;t = 1.5$ s
Notes

Meeting 5 · I Do Example 1 · Finding the Gradient

$x = 2t, \quad y = t^3 - t$  — find $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$
Step 1
$\dfrac{dx}{dt} = 2$
Step 2
$\dfrac{dy}{dt} = 3t^2 - 1$
Divide
$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{3t^2 - 1}{2}$
🧘 The gradient is given in terms of $t$ — that is normal for parametric curves.
Teacher Working

Meeting 5 · I Do Example 2 · Tangent (and Normal) at t = 2

Same curve: the tangent at $t = 2$
Point
$x = 2(2) = 4,\quad y = 2^3 - 2 = 6 \;\Rightarrow\; (4, 6)$
Gradient
$m = \dfrac{3(2)^2 - 1}{2} = \dfrac{11}{2}$
Tangent
$y - 6 = \dfrac{11}{2}(x - 4) \;\Rightarrow\;$ $2y = 11x - 32$
Normal
$m_n = -\dfrac{2}{11}$:  $11(y - 6) = -2(x - 4) \;\Rightarrow\;$ $2x + 11y = 74$
Teacher Working

Meeting 5 · You Do Worksheet 5

Worksheet 5 Independent exercise · 40 min

  • ① Find $\frac{dx}{dt}$ and $\frac{dy}{dt}$ (or $\frac{dx}{d\theta}$, $\frac{dy}{d\theta}$).
  • ② Divide: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}$.
  • ③ Substitute the value of $t$: point AND gradient.
  • ④ Tangent: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ · Normal: $m_n = -\frac{1}{m}$.
$\theta$ example: $x = 3\cos\theta,\ y = 2\sin\theta \Rightarrow \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2\cos\theta}{-3\sin\theta} = -\frac23\cot\theta$
