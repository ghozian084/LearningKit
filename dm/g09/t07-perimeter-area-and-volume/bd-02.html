Meeting 1 · Lecture Fence or Fertiliser?

Hook 🌾

A farmer puts a fence around a sawah and spreads fertiliser on the soil inside. Which is perimeter? Which is area?

Perimeter (1D) vs Area (2D)

Perimeter = distance around the shape → cm, m

Area = space inside the shape → cm², m²

Rectangle

$P = 2(l + w)$     $A = l \times w$

Units: cm, cm²

Meeting 1 · Lecture Parallelogram & Triangle

❓ We only know $A = l \times w$. How can we prove the area of a parallelogram and a triangle?

Parallelogram

Cut a right triangle off one end and slide it across: a rectangle!

$A = b \times h$

Triangle

Two identical triangles make a parallelogram, so one triangle is half:  $A = \frac12 b \times h$

⚠️ $h$ is the perpendicular height — never the slanted side.
Draw the Cut

Meeting 1 · I Do Example 1 · The Sawah

A rectangular sawah is 45 m by 28 m. Find the length of fence and the area to fertilise.
Perimeter
$P = 2(l + w) = 2(45 + 28) = 2 \times 73$
Answer
$P = 146$ m   (1D — metres)
Area
$A = l \times w = 45 \times 28$
Answer
$A = 1260$ m²   (2D — square metres)
Teacher Working

Meeting 1 · I Do Example 2 · Parallelogram & Triangle

Parallelogram: base 9 cm, slanted side 5 cm, perpendicular height 4 cm.
Perimeter
add all four sides: $P = 2(9 + 5) = $ 28 cm
Area
$A = b \times h = 9 \times 4 = $ 36 cm²
Trap
❌ $9 \times 5 = 45$ uses the slanted side — wrong!
Triangle: base 10 cm, perpendicular height 7 cm.
Area
$A = \frac12 \times 10 \times 7 = $ 35 cm²

Meeting 2 · Lecture Trapeziums & Composite Shapes

Hook 🏠

An L-shaped room needs carpet. There is no single formula — split it into rectangles, or subtract the missing corner.

Trapezium

A diagonal makes two triangles with the same height $h$:

$\frac12 ah + \frac12 bh = \frac12(a + b)h$

$A = \frac12(a + b)h$

"half the sum of the parallel sides × the height"

💡 Composite shapes: add the pieces, or subtract the holes — both give the same answer.
Notes

Meeting 2 · I Do Example 1 · A Trapezium

Trapezium: parallel sides 5 cm and 11 cm, height 6 cm.
Formula
$A = \frac12(a + b)h$
Substitute
$A = \frac12(5 + 11) \times 6 = \frac12 \times 16 \times 6$
Answer
$A = 48$ cm²
✅ Check with two triangles: $\frac12(5)(6) + \frac12(11)(6) = 15 + 33 = 48$ ✔
Teacher Working

Meeting 2 · I Do Example 2 · The L-Shaped Room

Find the area of carpet: a 12 m × 9 m room with a 5 m × 4 m corner missing.
Subtract
$12 \times 9 - 5 \times 4 = 108 - 20 = $ 88 m²
Split
bottom $12 \times 5 = 60$, top-left $7 \times 4 = 28$: $\;60 + 28 = $ 88 m² ✔
Teacher Working

Meeting 2 · We Do Mini-Whiteboards

✍️ Formula → substitute → units. Boards up on 3 – 2 – 1!

(a) Trapezium: parallel sides 8 cm and 14 cm, height 5 cm
(a)
$A = \frac12(8 + 14) \times 5 = \frac12 \times 22 \times 5 = $ 55 cm²
(b) Working backwards: a trapezium has area 60 cm² and parallel sides 7 cm and 13 cm. Find its height.
(b)
$\frac12(7 + 13)h = 60 \Rightarrow 10h = 60 \Rightarrow $ $h = 6$ cm

Meeting 3 · Lecture π and the Circumference

Hook 🧺

A round tampah needs rattan around the rim and a banana leaf on top. A circle has no straight sides — how do we measure it?

$\pi$

Measure any circle: circumference ÷ diameter $\approx 3.14$ — always! This constant is $\pi = 3.14159\ldots$

Circumference

$C = \pi d = 2\pi r$

$d$ = diameter (across), $r$ = radius (centre to edge), $d = 2r$

Notes

Meeting 3 · Lecture Area, Arcs & Sectors

Area

Cut a circle into thin sectors and lay them top-to-tail: almost a rectangle, $\pi r$ long and $r$ high.

$A = \pi r \times r = \pi r^2$

Sector with angle $\theta$ = the fraction $\frac{\theta}{360}$ of the circle

arc $= \frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$    area $= \frac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2$

⚠️ The perimeter of a sector or semicircle = curve + the straight edges.
Notes

Meeting 3 · I Do Example 1 · A Whole Circle

A circle has radius 6 cm. Find its circumference and its area (1 d.p.).
Circumference
$C = 2\pi r = 2 \times \pi \times 6 = 12\pi$
Answer
$C = 12\pi \approx 37.7$ cm
Area
$A = \pi r^2 = \pi \times 6^2 = 36\pi$
Answer
$A = 36\pi \approx 113.1$ cm²
⚠️ $6^2 = 36$, not $12$ — square the radius before multiplying by $\pi$.
Teacher Working

Meeting 3 · I Do Example 2 · A Sector

A sector has radius 10 cm and angle 72°. Find the arc length, the area and the perimeter.
Fraction
$\frac{72}{360} = \frac15$ of the circle
Arc
$\frac15 \times 2\pi \times 10 = 4\pi \approx $ 12.6 cm
Area
$\frac15 \times \pi \times 10^2 = 20\pi \approx $ 62.8 cm²
Perimeter
arc + two radii $= 4\pi + 20 \approx $ 32.6 cm
Teacher Working

Meeting 3 · We Do Mini-Whiteboards

✍️ Which formula? Then substitute. Boards up on 3 – 2 – 1!

(a) Semicircle, diameter 8 cm: area and perimeter
(a)
$r = 4$: $A = \frac12\pi(4)^2 = 8\pi \approx $ 25.1 cm²   $P = \frac12(8\pi) + 8 = 4\pi + 8 \approx $ 20.6 cm
(b) Working backwards: a circle has circumference 50 cm. Find its diameter.
(b)
$\pi d = 50 \Rightarrow d = \frac{50}{\pi} \approx $ 15.9 cm
Common Mistakes

Meeting 3 · You Do Worksheet 3

Worksheet 3 Independent practice · 30 min

  • $C = 2\pi r$ (cm) · $A = \pi r^2$ (cm²).
  • Semicircles and sectors: the perimeter includes the straight edges.
  • Give answers to 1 d.p.; exit ticket: the tampah (diameter 50 cm).

Meeting 4 · I Do Example 1 · Net of a Cuboid

Draw the net of a cuboid 5 cm × 3 cm × 2 cm and label every face.
Pairs
Opposite faces are equal: two of each.
Faces
$5 \times 3$ (top and bottom) · $5 \times 2$ (front and back) · $3 \times 2$ (two ends)
✅ Fold it in your head: every edge must meet an edge of the same length.

Meeting 4 · I Do Example 2 · Net of a Cylinder

A cylinder has radius 3 cm and height 10 cm. What shapes are in its net, and how long is the rectangle?
Shapes
two circles (top and bottom) + one rectangle (the curved side, unrolled)
Length
the rectangle wraps once around the circle, so its length = circumference $= 2\pi r$
Answer
$2\pi \times 3 = 6\pi \approx$ 18.8 cm by 10 cm
Teacher Working

(b)

Meeting 5 · Lecture Surface Area

Hook 🎁

Wrapping paper costs Rp 5 000 per m². How much paper does a gift box need — without waste?

Total surface area = area of the net

Find the area of every face, then add them up.

SolidTotal surface area
Cuboid $l \times w \times h$$2(lw + lh + wh)$
Cylinder$2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh$  (two circles + curved side)
Prism$2 \times$ cross-section $+$ perimeter of cross-section $\times$ length
⚠️ Surface area is an area: cm² or m² — not cm³.
Notes

Meeting 5 · I Do Example 1 · The Gift Box

A gift box is 40 cm × 30 cm × 20 cm. Paper costs Rp 5 000 per m². Find the cost of paper to cover it.
Faces
$40 \times 30 = 1200$,   $40 \times 20 = 800$,   $30 \times 20 = 600$ (two of each)
TSA
$2(1200 + 800 + 600) = 2 \times 2600 = 5200$ cm²
Convert
$1$ m² $= 100 \times 100 = 10\,000$ cm², so $5200$ cm² $= 0.52$ m²
Cost
$0.52 \times 5000 = $ Rp 2 600
Teacher Working

Meeting 5 · I Do Example 2 · A Cylinder

A cylinder has radius 4 cm and height 9 cm. Find its total surface area (1 d.p.).
Circles
$2\pi r^2 = 2\pi(4)^2 = 32\pi$
Curved
$2\pi rh = 2\pi(4)(9) = 72\pi$   (the rectangle in the net)
Total
$32\pi + 72\pi = 104\pi \approx $ 326.7 cm²
💡 Keep answers in terms of $\pi$ until the last step — it avoids rounding errors.
Teacher Working

(b) A cylinder with radius 2 cm and height 5 cm
(b)

Meeting 6 · Lecture Volume

🥩 "Surface area is the skin. Volume is the meat." How much space is inside?

Prisms and cylinders: stack the cross-section

$V = \text{area of cross-section} \times \text{length}$  ·  cylinder $V = \pi r^2 h$

Pyramids and cones

Rice from 3 pyramids exactly fills a prism with the same base and height:

$V = \frac13 \times \text{base area} \times h$  ·  cone $V = \frac13\pi r^2 h$

💡 Units: cm³ · and $1$ litre $= 1000$ cm³.
Notes

Meeting 6 · I Do Example 1 · Prism & Cylinder

(i) A triangular prism: cross-section base 6 cm and height 4 cm, length 15 cm.
Area
cross-section $= \frac12 \times 6 \times 4 = 12$ cm²
Volume
$V = 12 \times 15 = $ 180 cm³
(ii) A cylinder: radius 3 cm, height 10 cm.
Volume
$V = \pi r^2 h = \pi \times 9 \times 10 = 90\pi \approx $ 282.7 cm³
Teacher Working

Meeting 6 · I Do Example 2 · Cone & Pyramid

(i) A cone: radius 5 cm, height 12 cm.
Volume
$V = \frac13\pi r^2 h = \frac13 \times \pi \times 25 \times 12 = 100\pi \approx $ 314.2 cm³
(ii) A square-based pyramid: base 6 cm, height 10 cm.
Volume
$V = \frac13 \times 6^2 \times 10 = \frac13 \times 360 = $ 120 cm³
⚠️ Use the perpendicular height of the cone or pyramid, not the slant height.
Teacher Working

Meeting 6 · We Do Mini-Whiteboards

✍️ Boards up on 3 – 2 – 1!

(a) A cone and a cylinder have the same radius and height. The cylinder holds 300 cm³. The cone?
(a)
$\frac13 \times 300 = $ 100 cm³
(b) A fish tank is 50 cm × 30 cm × 40 cm. How many litres?
(b)
$60\,000$ cm³ $\div 1000 = $ 60 litres
(c) A cylinder has volume $200\pi$ cm³ and radius 5 cm. Find its height.
(c)
$25\pi h = 200\pi \Rightarrow $ $h = 8$ cm

Meeting 7 · Lecture Peeling the Orange

Hook 🍊

Draw 4 circles with the orange's radius. The peel fills exactly 4 of them!

Sphere

surface area $= 4\pi r^2$     volume $= \frac43\pi r^3$

A ball has radius 5 cm.
Surface
$4\pi(5)^2 = 100\pi \approx $ 314.2 cm²
Volume
$\frac43\pi(5)^3 = \frac{500}{3}\pi \approx $ 523.6 cm³
Notes

Meeting 7 · I Do Example · A Hemisphere

A solid hemisphere has radius 4 cm. Find its total surface area and its volume (1 d.p.).
Curved
half a sphere: $\frac12 \times 4\pi r^2 = 2\pi(4)^2 = 32\pi$
Flat
the circular base: $\pi r^2 = 16\pi$
Total
$32\pi + 16\pi = 48\pi \approx $ 150.8 cm²
Volume
$\frac12 \times \frac43\pi(4)^3 = \frac{128}{3}\pi \approx $ 134.0 cm³
⚠️ Don't forget the flat circle on a solid hemisphere.
