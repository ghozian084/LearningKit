🎮 Simulation: Testing the Compound Angle Formulae

Move $A$ and $B$. The "naive" guess $\sin A + \sin B$ almost never works — the formula always does.

45° 30°

📝 Step-by-Step Example: exact $\sin 75^\circ$

Step 1: $75^\circ = 45^\circ + 30^\circ$, so use $\sin(A + B) = \sin A\cos B + \cos A\sin B$.
Step 2: $\sin 75^\circ = \sin 45^\circ\cos 30^\circ + \cos 45^\circ\sin 30^\circ$
Step 3: $= \frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2} + \frac{\sqrt2}{2}\cdot\frac12 = \frac{\sqrt6}{4} + \frac{\sqrt2}{4}$
Step 4: $\sin 75^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt6 + \sqrt2}{4} \approx 0.9659$ ✅ (check with the simulation: $A = 45$, $B = 30$)

📝 Step-by-Step Example: prove $\sec\theta - \cos\theta \equiv \sin\theta\tan\theta$

Step 1: Start with the LHS and rewrite in $\cos$: $\sec\theta - \cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{\cos\theta} - \cos\theta$
Step 2: Common denominator: $= \dfrac{1 - \cos^2\theta}{\cos\theta}$
Step 3: Pythagoras: $= \dfrac{\sin^2\theta}{\cos\theta} = \sin\theta \times \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$
Step 4: $= \sin\theta\tan\theta = $ RHS $\blacksquare$
