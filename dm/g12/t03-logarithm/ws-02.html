Focus: Understanding Linear Forms & Designing Your Experiment

Background: Transforming to Linear Form ($y = A e^{kt}$)

Real-world exponential relationships (like cooling, decay, or growth) are often modeled by $y = A e^{kt}$. Plotting $y$ vs $t$ gives a curve. To find $A$ and $k$ from a graph, we need a straight line!
Step 1: Take $\ln$ of both sides: $\ln y = \ln(A e^{kt})$
Step 2: Use Product Law: $\ln y = \ln A + \ln(e^{kt})$
Step 3: Simplify: $\ln y = kt + \ln A$
This is now in the form $Y = mX + C$!
So, you will plot: $Y = \ln y$ (vertical axis) against $X = t$ (horizontal axis).
