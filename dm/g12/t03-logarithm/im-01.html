🎮 Simulation: Exponentials vs Logarithms

A logarithm is the mathematical inverse of an exponential. Watch how they reflect across the line $y=x$. Adjust the Base ($a$)!

$y = a^x$ $y = \log_a(x)$ Mirror: $y = x$

📝 Step-by-Step Example

Problem: Convert the equation $5^3 = 125$ into logarithmic form.

Step 1: Identify the Base. The base of the exponent is 5. This becomes the base of the log.
Step 2: The Logarithm isolates the power. The power is 3. So, the equation will equal 3.
Step 3: Assemble the formula: $\log_5(125) = 3$.

🎮 Simulation: The Product Law

Before calculators, astronomers added logarithms to multiply massive numbers! Let's prove $\log_2(A) + \log_2(B) = \log_2(A \times B)$.

Let $A = 8$ and $B = 4$.
$\log_2(8) = 3$
$\log_2(4) = 2$
Add them: $3 + 2 = 5$
Now calculate $\log_2(8 \times 4) = \log_2(32)$.
$2^5 = 32$, so $\log_2(32) = 5$.
The results match! Addition of logs = Multiplication of values.

📝 Step-by-Step Example

Problem: Write $2\log_a x - \log_a y$ as a single logarithm.

Step 1: Use the Power Law to move the coefficient. $2\log_a x \rightarrow \log_a(x^2)$.
Step 2: Substitute back into the expression: $\log_a(x^2) - \log_a y$.
Step 3: Use the Quotient Law for subtraction: $\log_a \left(\frac{x^2}{y}\right)$.

🎮 Concept: Bringing the Power Down

When the unknown ($x$) is trapped in the exponent, we cannot divide to get it out. We must take the log (or natural log, $\ln$) of both sides!

Solve: $3^x = 20$

1. $\ln(3^x) = \ln(20)$
2. $x \cdot \ln(3) = \ln(20)$   (Power Law drops the x!)
3. $x = \frac{\ln(20)}{\ln(3)}$
4. $x \approx 2.727$

📝 Step-by-Step Example

Problem: Solve the inequality $0.5^x < 10$.

Step 1: Take $\log$ of both sides. $\log(0.5^x) < \log(10)$.
Step 2: Use the power law. $x \log(0.5) < \log(10)$.
Step 3: Divide by $\log(0.5)$. CRITICAL: $\log(0.5)$ is a negative number! When dividing by a negative, the inequality sign MUST FLIP.
Step 4: $x > \frac{\log 10}{\log 0.5} \implies x > -3.32$.

🎮 Simulation: Straightening the Cooling Curve

Your Teh Panas cools down following the exponential model $y = Ae^{kt}$.
Click "Transform Data" to take the Natural Log ($\ln$) of the y-axis and straighten the curve into $Y = mX + C$!

Model: $y = 50e^{-0.1t}$

📝 Step-by-Step Example

Problem: Convert $y = k x^n$ to a linear form.

Step 1: Take $\log$ of both sides. $\log y = \log(k x^n)$.
Step 2: Use Product Law to split the right side. $\log y = \log k + \log(x^n)$.
Step 3: Use Power Law to bring $n$ down. $\log y = n\log x + \log k$.
This is now the line $Y = mX + C$, where $Y = \log y$, $X = \log x$, gradient $m = n$, and intercept $C = \log k$.
