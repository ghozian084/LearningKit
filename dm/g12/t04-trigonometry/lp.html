Lesson Plan: Trigonometry

School: SMA Semesta 2026/2025 | Grade: 12

Time Allocation: 6 Meetings (2 x 40 minutes = 80 mins per meeting)

📌 Topic: 3. Trigonometry

Reference: Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics: Pure Mathematics 2 & 3

Method: Problem Based Learning (PBL) integrated with the 5E Model

Approach: Hands-on Activity & Deep Learning (Pembelajaran Mendalam: Mindful, Meaningful, Joyful)

Character Focus: Self-Regulated, Creativity, Critical Thinking

Differentiation: Extension, Scaffolding, EAL, SEN

🎯 Objectives

  • 2.3.1 / 3.3.1: Understand and use the definitions of cosecant, secant, and cotangent, and their relationships to sine, cosine, and tangent.
  • 2.3.2 / 3.3.2: Use the compound angle formulae for sine, cosine, and tangent.
  • 2.3.3 / 3.3.3: Use the double angle formulae for sine, cosine, and tangent.
  • 2.3.4 / 3.3.4: Prove and use further trigonometric identities, including those involving cosecant, secant, cotangent, and sums and differences of angles.
  • 2.3.5 / 3.3.5: Express $a\sin\theta + b\cos\theta$ in the form $R\sin(\theta \pm \alpha)$ or $R\cos(\theta \pm \alpha)$, and use this form to solve equations and find maximum and minimum values.
WALT — We Are Learning To...

Learning Objective:

Use reciprocal ratios, compound and double angle formulae, trigonometric identities and the $R$-formula to model and solve real wave and angle problems — ending with a safe-docking plan for a ship using a tide model.


Bloom's Level:

Apply, Analyse & Evaluate (Levels 3–5), with Create in the Tide Report

WILT — What I'm Looking For...

Success Criteria (self-assessable):

  1. I can use $\sec$, $\operatorname{cosec}$ and $\cot$ and the identities $1 + \tan^2\theta \equiv \sec^2\theta$ and $1 + \cot^2\theta \equiv \operatorname{cosec}^2\theta$.
  2. I can use the compound angle formulae to find exact values and solve equations.
  3. I can use the double angle formulae, choosing the right form of $\cos 2A$.
  4. I can prove identities with clear, logical steps (one side to the other).
  5. I can write $a\sin\theta + b\cos\theta$ as $R\sin(\theta \pm \alpha)$ or $R\cos(\theta \pm \alpha)$ and use it for maximum/minimum values and equations.
🌊 PBL Driving Problem: "The Tanjung Priok Tide Challenge"
The port authority models the tide height by $h(t) = 1.2 + 0.6\sin(30t)^\circ + 0.8\cos(30t)^\circ$ metres, where $t$ is the number of hours after midnight. A cargo ship needs $h \ge 1.8$ m to dock safely. When should the ship dock, and what is the highest tide? Each meeting gives every student one more trigonometric tool; in Meeting 5 each student solves the problem and writes an individual Tide Report, which is presented in Meeting 6.
Learning organisation: students work individually (with short "turn & check" moments with a neighbour). Every meeting follows the PBL syntax inside a full 5E cycle:

📅 Meeting 1: The Cosecant, Secant and Cotangent Ratios (Subtopic 3.1)

ENGAGE · PBL 1 Orientation — The School Ramp[10 mins]
Teacher-led Hook
  • Meaningful: A wheelchair ramp must rise 0.5 m at no more than $4.8^\circ$. Students find the ramp length $L = \frac{0.5}{\sin\theta}$ and notice the ratio $\frac{1}{\sin\theta}$ keeps appearing — it deserves its own name.
  • Introduce the chapter driving problem (the Tanjung Priok tide) and show the tide graph.
EXPLORE · PBL 2 — Measuring the Unit Circle[20 mins]
Individual Hands-On Investigation
  • Joyful: Each student constructs tangent lines on the printed unit circle (Hands-on 1, $\theta = 35^\circ$) and measures the lengths that represent $\tan\theta$, $\sec\theta$, $\cot\theta$ and $\operatorname{cosec}\theta$, then compares them with $\frac{1}{\cos\theta}$, $\frac{1}{\sin\theta}$, $\frac{1}{\tan\theta}$ on a calculator.
  • Spot the right triangle $OAT$: $OT^2 = 1 + AT^2$, i.e. $\sec^2\theta = 1 + \tan^2\theta$.
EXPLAIN · PBL 3 — Definitions, Graphs & Pythagorean Identities[20 mins]
Direct Instruction & Guided Practice
  • $\sec\theta = \frac{1}{\cos\theta}$, $\operatorname{cosec}\theta = \frac{1}{\sin\theta}$, $\cot\theta = \frac{1}{\tan\theta} = \frac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}$; exact values for $30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ$.
  • Sketch $y = \sec x$, $y = \operatorname{cosec} x$, $y = \cot x$ from their parent graphs (asymptotes where the parent is zero). Use the Web Lab, Tab 1.
  • Derive $1 + \tan^2\theta \equiv \sec^2\theta$ and $1 + \cot^2\theta \equiv \operatorname{cosec}^2\theta$ by dividing $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta \equiv 1$.
ELABORATE · PBL 4 — Solving with Reciprocal Ratios[20 mins]
Individual Application
  • Worksheet 1: equations such as $\sec\theta = 2$, exact values in other quadrants, and the quadratic $2\tan^2\theta + \sec\theta = 1$.
  • Scaffold: "Flip first" — rewrite every reciprocal ratio as $\sin$, $\cos$ or $\tan$ before solving.
EVALUATE · PBL 5 — Exit Ticket[10 mins]
Individual Assessment
  • Mindful: Exit ticket — solve $\operatorname{cosec} x = 3$ for $0^\circ \le x \le 360^\circ$; self-rate WILT 1.

📅 Meeting 2: Compound Angle Formulae (Subtopic 3.2)

ENGAGE · PBL 1 Orientation — Is $\sin(A+B) = \sin A + \sin B$?[10 mins]
Discussion & Prediction
  • Critical Thinking: Test $\sin(30^\circ + 60^\circ)$ against $\sin 30^\circ + \sin 60^\circ$. A Joglo roof designer needs the exact value of $\sin 75^\circ$ without a calculator — how?
EXPLORE · PBL 2 — The Three-Triangle Proof Kit[25 mins]
Individual Hands-On Investigation
  • Joyful: Each student cuts the three scaled triangles ($A = 30^\circ$, $B = 40^\circ$) from Hands-on 2 and assembles them on the mat. The height of the top vertex is $\sin(A+B)$ — and it splits into $\sin A\cos B + \cos A\sin B$.
  • Verify numerically for other angle pairs (Worksheet 2 table, Web Lab Tab 2).
EXPLAIN · PBL 3 — The Six Formulae[15 mins]
Direct Instruction
  • $\sin(A \pm B)$, $\cos(A \pm B)$ (note the sign swap!), $\tan(A \pm B) = \frac{\tan A \pm \tan B}{1 \mp \tan A\tan B}$. Exact values: $\sin 75^\circ = \frac{\sqrt6 + \sqrt2}{4}$, $\tan 75^\circ = 2 + \sqrt3$.
ELABORATE · PBL 4 — Exact Values & Equations[20 mins]
Individual Application
  • Worksheet 2: $\sin(A+B)$ from given ratios, solve $\sin(\theta + 30^\circ) = 2\cos\theta$ and $\tan(x + 45^\circ) = 2$, prove $\cos(A+B) + \cos(A-B) \equiv 2\cos A\cos B$.
  • Scaffold: formula card with colour-coded signs (Hands-on 2, page 2).
EVALUATE · PBL 5 — Exit Ticket[10 mins]
Individual Assessment
  • Find the exact value of $\cos 75^\circ$. Self-rate WILT 2.

📅 Meeting 3: Double Angle Formulae (Subtopic 3.3)

ENGAGE · PBL 1 Orientation — The Perfect Sepak Takraw Kick[10 mins]
Visual Hook
  • Meaningful: The range of a kicked ball is $R = \frac{v^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}$. Students tabulate $R$ for $15^\circ$ to $75^\circ$ ($v = 10$, $g = 10$) and ask: why do $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ give the same range, and why is $45^\circ$ best?
EXPLORE · PBL 2 — Cut-and-Rearrange Proof[20 mins]
Individual Hands-On Investigation
  • Joyful: Cut the isosceles triangle (apex $2\theta$, equal sides 1) from Hands-on 3 along its altitude. Its area is $\frac12\sin 2\theta$, but the two halves also form a rectangle of area $\sin\theta\cos\theta$ — so $\sin 2\theta = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$.
  • Set $B = A$ in the compound formulae to derive all three double angle formulae.
EXPLAIN · PBL 3 — Three Faces of $\cos 2A$[15 mins]
Direct Instruction
  • $\cos 2A = \cos^2 A - \sin^2 A = 2\cos^2 A - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2 A$; $\tan 2A = \frac{2\tan A}{1 - \tan^2 A}$; rearranged forms $\cos^2 A = \frac{1 + \cos 2A}{2}$, $\sin^2 A = \frac{1 - \cos 2A}{2}$.
  • Card sort (Hands-on 3, page 2): which form of $\cos 2A$ makes each equation a single-ratio quadratic?
ELABORATE · PBL 4 — Solving Double Angle Equations[25 mins]
Individual Application
  • Worksheet 3: $\cos 2x = \sin x$, $\sin 2\theta = \cos\theta$, and the takraw angles that give a 7 m range.
  • Scaffold: never divide by $\cos\theta$ — factorise instead, or solutions are lost!
EVALUATE · PBL 5 — Exit Ticket[10 mins]
Individual Assessment
  • Given $\cos A = \frac13$, find $\cos 2A$. Self-rate WILT 3.

📅 Meeting 4: Further Trigonometric Identities (Subtopic 3.4)

ENGAGE · PBL 1 Orientation — Two Engineers, Two Formulas[10 mins]
Discussion
  • Critical Thinking: One engineer uses $\frac{1 - \cos 2\theta}{\sin 2\theta}$, another uses $\tan\theta$. Testing $\theta = 20^\circ$ gives the same answer. Is that enough? What makes a proof different from checking examples?
EXPLORE · PBL 2 — Proof Puzzles[20 mins]
Individual Hands-On Investigation
  • Joyful: Each student cuts and re-orders the shuffled proof strips (Hands-on 4) for four identities. Each puzzle contains one trap strip that is mathematically wrong — find it and explain why.
  • Use the identity checker (Web Lab, Tab 4) to see that both sides have the same graph.
EXPLAIN · PBL 3 — Proof Strategies[15 mins]
Direct Instruction
  • Start from the more complicated side; rewrite in $\sin$/$\cos$; use Pythagorean and double angle identities; combine fractions; factorise (difference of two squares). Never "move terms across" an identity you are proving.
  • Solving equations in $\sec$, $\operatorname{cosec}$, $\cot$ by turning them into quadratics with one ratio.
ELABORATE · PBL 4 — Prove & Solve[25 mins]
Individual Application
  • Worksheet 4: five proofs of increasing difficulty and the equations $3\cot^2\theta - 5\operatorname{cosec}\theta + 1 = 0$ and $2\sec^2\theta = 3\tan\theta + 1$.
  • Scaffold: identity "toolbox" card listing every identity met so far.
EVALUATE · PBL 5 — Exit Ticket[10 mins]
Individual Assessment
  • Prove $\tan\theta + \cot\theta \equiv \sec\theta\operatorname{cosec}\theta$. Self-rate WILT 4.

📅 Meeting 5: $a\sin\theta + b\cos\theta = R\sin(\theta \pm \alpha)$ — Solving the Tide Problem (Subtopic 3.5)

ENGAGE · PBL 1 Orientation — Back to Tanjung Priok[10 mins]
Problem Re-launch
  • Meaningful: Revisit the tide model. From a table of $h(t)$ values students estimate the highest tide — but where exactly is the maximum, and when is $h \ge 1.8$?
EXPLORE · PBL 2 — Adding Two Waves[20 mins]
Individual Hands-On Investigation
  • Joyful: On the printed graph (Hands-on 5) students add the ordinates of $y = 3\sin x$ and $y = 4\cos x$ point by point. The sum is a single sine wave with amplitude 5, shifted left by about $53^\circ$.
  • Use the wave combiner (Web Lab, Tab 5) to test other values of $a$ and $b$.
EXPLAIN · PBL 3 — The $R$-Formula Method[15 mins]
Direct Instruction
  • Expand $R\sin(\theta + \alpha)$ and compare coefficients: $R\cos\alpha = a$, $R\sin\alpha = b$, so $R = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$ and $\tan\alpha = \frac{b}{a}$. Choose $\sin(\theta \pm \alpha)$ or $\cos(\theta \pm \alpha)$ to keep $R > 0$ and $\alpha$ acute.
  • Maximum $= R$, minimum $= -R$; solving $a\sin\theta + b\cos\theta = c$ becomes one sine equation (adjust the interval for $\theta + \alpha$).
ELABORATE · PBL 4 — Develop the Tide Report[25 mins]
Individual Product
  • Each student writes $0.6\sin x + 0.8\cos x = \sin(x + 53.13^\circ)$, finds the highest tide (2.2 m at about 01:14), the lowest tide (0.2 m at about 07:14) and the safe docking windows (00:00–03:00 and from about 11:27), and completes the Tide Report (Hands-on 5, page 2).
  • Scaffold: step-by-step $R$-formula "recipe" card; Extension: find the safe windows for a ship that needs 2.0 m.
EVALUATE · PBL 5 — Exit Ticket[10 mins]
Individual Assessment
  • Express $\sqrt3\sin\theta + \cos\theta$ in the form $R\sin(\theta + \alpha)$. Self-rate WILT 5.

📅 Meeting 6: Present, Review & Chapter 3 Evaluation

PBL 4 — Presenting the Tide Reports[10 mins]
Student Presentations
  • Three students present their docking recommendation (2–3 minutes each); the class checks the $R$, $\alpha$ and the safe windows. Teacher assesses Tide Reports with the rubric (Appendix).
Review — "I Have… Who Has…?" Loop[10 mins]
Whole-Class Game
  • Joyful: 20 loop cards (Hands-on 6) covering every subtopic; the chain must return to the START card. Time the class.
EVALUATE · PBL 5 — Chapter 3 Test & Reflection[60 mins]
Summative Test
  • Chapter 3 Test (Worksheet 6, 55 min, 50 marks) covering all five subtopics.
  • Mindful: Reflection (5 min): "Which identity was the most useful tool in solving the tide problem, and why?" Self-rate all five WILT criteria.

Appendix: PBL Driving Problem, Tide Report Rubric & Teacher Notes

🌊 The Tanjung Priok Tide Challenge (student brief)

The tide height at the port is modelled by $h(t) = 1.2 + 0.6\sin(30t)^\circ + 0.8\cos(30t)^\circ$ metres, $0 \le t \le 12$ hours after midnight. A cargo ship needs at least 1.8 m of water above the reference level to dock safely.

  1. Express $0.6\sin x + 0.8\cos x$ in the form $R\sin(x + \alpha)$.
  2. Find the highest and lowest tide and the times they occur.
  3. Find all times between 00:00 and 12:00 when it is safe to dock.
  4. Write a short recommendation for the harbour master, with a sketch of $h(t)$.

Teacher answers: $R = 1$, $\alpha = 53.13^\circ$; max 2.2 m at $t \approx 1.23$ h (01:14); min 0.2 m at $t \approx 7.23$ h (07:14); safe when $0 \le t \le 3$ and $11.46 \le t \le 12$ (00:00–03:00 and 11:27–12:00).

💡 Teacher Tips for Execution

  1. Explore before Explain: let students build the unit circle, the three-triangle kit and the wave graph before naming the formulae — the Explain phase then starts from their observations.
  2. Degrees vs radians: keep calculators in degree mode for this chapter; remind students when an interval is given in radians.
  3. Lost solutions: insist on factorising rather than dividing by $\cos\theta$ or $\sin\theta$ (Meetings 3–4).
  4. Proof Puzzle key (Hands-on 4): Puzzle ①: B → F → D → A → G → C (trap E). Puzzle ②: D → B → F → E → A (trap C). Puzzle ③: E → A → F → B → D (trap C). Puzzle ④: C → E → B → D → F (trap A).
  5. Printing: every worksheet and hands-on file is laid out as full A4 pages, one file per meeting — print double-sided with no wasted paper.
