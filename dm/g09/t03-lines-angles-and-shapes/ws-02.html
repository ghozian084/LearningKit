1. Basic Concept: Special Quadrilaterals

The 4 interior angles of ANY quadrilateral sum to $360^\circ$.

  • Parallelogram: Opposite angles are EQUAL. Adjacent angles sum to $180^\circ$ (C-shape rule).
  • Rhombus: All sides equal. Diagonals cross exactly at $90^\circ$.
  • Kite: Adjacent sides equal. Exactly ONE pair of opposite angles is equal.
2. Example Question and Solution

Question: In a parallelogram, angle A is $70^\circ$. Find the adjacent angle B and opposite angle C.

Solution:
Opposite angles are equal $\implies$ Angle C = $70^\circ$.
Adjacent angles sum to $180^\circ \implies$ Angle B = $180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ$.

4. Case: Diagonals forming Triangles

If you draw a diagonal across a quadrilateral, it splits the shape into two triangles. Inside a Rhombus or Kite, these diagonals cross at $90^\circ$, creating 4 right-angled triangles! You can use triangle angle rules ($180^\circ$) to find missing pieces inside the shapes.

1. Basic Concept: Polygon Formulas

Interior Sum: $(n - 2) \times 180^\circ$ (where $n$ is the number of sides).
Exterior Sum: ALWAYS $360^\circ$ (for any convex polygon).
Regular Polygon: All sides and angles are equal.
ONE exterior angle = $\frac{360^\circ}{n}$.
Interior + Exterior = $180^\circ$.

2. Example Question and Solution

Question: Find the size of ONE interior angle of a regular pentagon ($n=5$).

Solution Method (Faster Exterior Rule):
Exterior angle = $360 \div 5 = 72^\circ$.
Interior angle = $180 - 72 = 108^\circ$.

4. Case: Non-Regular Polygons

If a polygon is NOT regular, you cannot simply divide by $n$ to find one angle. You must use the full formula $(n-2) \times 180$ to find the TOTAL sum, then subtract all the known angles to find the missing one!

1. Basic Concept: Circle Theorems
  • Semicircle: Angle in a semicircle is $90^\circ$.
  • Center/Circumference: Angle at the center is TWICE the angle at the circumference (from the same arc).
  • Cyclic Quadrilateral: Opposite angles sum to $180^\circ$. (All 4 corners must touch the edge).
2. Example Question and Solution

Question: The angle subtended by an arc at the center is $140^\circ$. Find the angle at the circumference.

Solution: The angle at the circumference is half the angle at the center. $140^\circ \div 2 = 70^\circ$.

4. Case: Finding the Hidden Isosceles Triangle

If you draw lines from the center of the circle to the edge, those lines are Radii. All radii in the same circle are equal in length! This means a triangle formed by two radii and a chord is ALWAYS an Isosceles Triangle. You can use this to find missing angles!

1. Basic Concept: Compass and Straightedge

In geometry construction, we don't guess angles. We use a compass to measure exact lengths and find intersections.
SSS Construction (Side-Side-Side):
1. Draw the longest base with a ruler.
2. Open your compass to the second length, place the point on one end, and draw an arc.
3. Repeat for the third length from the other end.
4. Connect the intersection down to the base.

2. Example Problem

Question: Construct a triangle with sides 4cm, 5cm, and 6cm.

Solution Strategy: Draw 6cm line. Set compass to 4cm, draw arc from left. Set compass to 5cm, draw arc from right. Connect the point where the arcs cross!

3. Guided Practice: Equilateral Triangle

Construct a perfect equilateral triangle with sides exactly 5cm long.

Step 1: Draw a straight line exactly 5cm long below.
Step 2: Open your compass to exactly 5cm. Place the spike on the left edge, draw a large arc above the line.
Step 3: Keep the compass at 5cm. Place the spike on the right edge, draw an arc crossing the first one. Connect the dots!
