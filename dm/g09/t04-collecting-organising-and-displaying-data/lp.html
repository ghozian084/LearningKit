Lesson Plan: Collecting, Organising and Displaying Data

School: SMP Semesta 2026/2025 | Grade: 9

Time Allocation: 6 Meetings (2 x 40 minutes = 80 mins per meeting)

📌 Topic: 4. Data Handling (Cambridge IGCSE)

Reference: Cambridge IGCSE® Math Core & Extended

Method: Project Based Learning (PjBL)

Approach: Hands-on Activity & Deep Learning

Character Focus: Health, Self-Regulated, Collaboration, Creativity, Critical Thinking

Differentiation: Extension, Scaffolding, EAL, SEN

🎯 Objectives

  • C9.1, E9.1: Classify and tabulate statistical data (categorical, numerical, discrete, continuous).
  • C9.4, E9.4: Draw and interpret statistical charts: bar charts (simple, dual, composite), pie charts, pictograms, line graphs.
  • C9.4, E9.4: Construct and interpret ordered stem-and-leaf diagrams and two-way tables.
WALT — We Are Learning To...

Learning Objective:

Follow the full statistical investigation process to classify, organise, and visually display health-related data using tables, stem-and-leaf diagrams, and advanced charts.


Bloom's Level:

Apply, Analyse & Create (Levels 3–6)

WILT — What I'm Looking For...

Success Criteria (self-assessable):

  1. I can correctly classify data as qualitative, discrete, or continuous.
  2. I can organise raw data using tally tables, class intervals, and two-way tables.
  3. I can construct ordered stem-and-leaf diagrams (including back-to-back) with a clear Key.
  4. I can draw accurate Bar Charts (dual and composite) and Pictograms.
  5. I can calculate sector angles to draw a precise Pie Chart and read trends from a Line Graph.
🏥 PjBL Theme: "The Semesta Healthy Lifestyles Dashboard"
Throughout these 6 meetings, students act as school health data analysts. They will design surveys, collect data from classmates on health metrics (e.g., hours of sleep, glasses of water per day, exercise frequency), and build a visual dashboard to present to the "Principal" (teacher) to advocate for better student health!

📅 Meeting 1: Classifying Data & The Investigation Cycle (Subtopic 4.1)

PjBL Phase 1 | Essential Question & Hook[35 mins]
Teacher-led & Class Discussion

Hook — The Health Crisis (15 min):

  • Health Context: Show a statistic on the board: "70% of teenagers are sleep deprived." Ask: "Is this true for our class? How can we prove it? We need DATA."

Project Launch & Concept (20 min):

  • Introduce the Statistical Investigation Cycle: 1) Identify question, 2) Collect, 3) Organise/Display, 4) Analyse/Interpret.
  • Direct Instruction: Types of data. Categorical (non-numerical/qualitative) vs Numerical (quantitative).
  • Numerical breakdown: Discrete (counted) vs Continuous (measured). Discuss edge cases: Age is continuous, Money is discrete!
PjBL Phase 2 | Designing the Plan[45 mins]
Collaborative Group Work

Designing the Survey (25 min):

  • Groups of 3 brainstorm health topics (See Appendix for specific group assignments).
  • Meaningful: Groups must draft 3 survey questions ensuring they have exactly ONE Categorical, ONE Discrete, and ONE Continuous question.
  • Discuss Primary sources (surveys/measuring) vs Secondary sources (internet databases).

Evaluate & Approve (20 min):

  • Teacher reviews group questions before approving them for data collection in the next meeting.

📅 Meeting 2: Organising Data - Tables & Intervals (Subtopic 4.2)

PjBL Phase 3 | Executing the Plan (Data Collection)[30 mins]
Hands-On Motoric Activity

Data Collection Mission (30 min):

  • Joyful: Students leave their desks. With clipboards in hand, groups must circulate the room and survey exactly 20 classmates.
  • Collaboration: One asks, one tallies, one organizes the flow.
  • Teacher monitors to ensure respectful and efficient data gathering.
PjBL Phase 4 | Organising the Raw Data[50 mins]
Direct Instruction & Practice

Direct Instruction — Tables (20 min):

  • Show how to transfer tally marks into Frequency Tables.
  • Introduce Class Intervals for continuous data (e.g., $150 \le h < 160$). Highlight that intervals must NOT overlap.
  • Introduce Two-Way Tables to summarize two sets of categorical data (e.g., Left/Right handed vs Class A/Class B).

Elaborate (30 min):

  • Groups convert their raw clipboard data into professional Frequency Tables and a Two-way table on A4 paper for their Dashboard.

📅 Meeting 3: Stem-and-Leaf Diagrams (Subtopic 4.2)

PjBL Phase 4 | Preserving the Data[35 mins]
Teacher-led & Hands-On

Hook — Preserving Raw Values (10 min):

  • "A frequency table hides the exact numbers. How can we group data but still see every single exact data point? Stem-and-Leaf!"

Physical Sorting Activity (25 min):

  • Provide envelopes with 25 pieces of numerical data.
  • Students physically arrange the paper numbers on their desks into a "stem" (tens) and "leaf" (units) format.
  • Mindful Check: "Are your leaves ordered smallest to largest? Did you include a Key ($7 | 2 = 72$)?"
PjBL Phase 4 | Complex Diagrams[45 mins]
Independent Practice

Elaborate (35 min):

  • Introduce Back-to-Back Stem-and-Leaf diagrams to compare two sets of data (e.g., Male vs Female resting heart rates).
  • Students complete Worksheet on constructing standard and back-to-back diagrams.

Evaluate (10 min):

  • Exit Ticket: Look at a back-to-back diagram. Read the largest value for Brand X and the smallest value for Brand Y.

📅 Meeting 4: Bar Charts & Pictograms (Subtopic 4.3)

PjBL Phase 4 | Visual Communication[35 mins]
Direct Instruction & Critique

Hook — Misleading Data (10 min):

  • Show a news clipping with a highly misleading bar chart (e.g., y-axis starting at 50 to exaggerate a tiny difference).
  • Critical Thinking: "How can we visually communicate health data accurately and honestly?"

Direct Instruction (25 min):

  • Pictograms: Must have a key! Discuss fractional symbols (e.g., if a clock = 1 hour, what does $\frac{3}{4}$ of a clock represent?).
  • Bar Charts: Rules! Equal width, equal gaps, labeled axes starting at zero.
  • Introduce Dual Bar Charts (side-by-side) and Composite Bar Charts (stacked to show parts of a whole).
PjBL Phase 4 | Dashboard Development[45 mins]
Collaborative Project Work

Drafting Project Artifacts (45 min):

  • Groups use their health survey data from M2.
  • They must draft one precise Bar Chart (Dual or Composite) and one Pictogram on A3 grid paper.
  • Teacher circulates, strictly enforcing graph rules (scale, gaps, keys).

📅 Meeting 5: Pie Charts & Line Graphs (Subtopic 4.3)

PjBL Phase 4 | Proportions & Time Trends[40 mins]
Direct Instruction & Hands-On

Pie Charts (20 min):

  • Formula: $\text{Angle} = \left(\frac{\text{Frequency}}{\text{Total}}\right) \times 360^\circ$.
  • Teacher models drawing slices with a protractor. "Always check your angles sum to $360^\circ$ before drawing!"

Line Graphs (20 min):

  • Explain that Line Graphs are strictly for showing changes over time (e.g., temperature over months, happiness level during a match).
PjBL Phase 4 | Final Assembly[40 mins]
Collaborative Project Work

Finalizing the Health Dashboard (40 min):

  • Groups calculate angles for their final dataset and draw a large Pie Chart onto their A3 Dashboard.
  • Groups glue their tables, bar charts, pictograms, and pie charts onto a master display board.
  • Self-Regulated: Final check against the success criteria checklist.

📅 Meeting 6: Showcase & Chapter Evaluation

PjBL Phase 5 | Presenting Solutions[30 mins]
Student-led Gallery Walk

Health Dashboard Gallery Walk (30 min):

  • Posters are displayed around the room.
  • Students evaluate peers' graphs using a checklist: Title/Key present? Scale accurate/Not misleading? Data correctly categorized?
  • Groups present a 1-minute summary of their Health Findings to the teacher.
PjBL Phase 6 | Evaluating the Experience (Summative)[50 mins]
Summative Test

Chapter 4 Evaluation (45 min):

  • Remove visual aids. Distribute the Chapter 4 Test covering data classification, stem-and-leaf, dual/composite bar charts, pie chart calculations, and interpreting line graphs.

Closure & Reflection (5 min):

  • Self-Reflection: "Did drawing conclusions from data change how you view your own health habits?"
🌟 Differentiation Strategies
Profile Strategies & Actions in Class
Extension (High Achievers) Require the use of Composite (stacked) bar charts and back-to-back stem-and-leaf diagrams in their projects. Challenge them to explain when a line graph is inappropriate (e.g., using it for categorical data like favorite colors).
Scaffolding (Middle/Low) Provide pre-drawn axes on graph paper for their bar charts. Provide a step-by-step checklist card for calculating Pie Chart angles. Ensure they understand "Discrete = Count, Continuous = Measure".
EAL (English Additional Language) Provide a visual, bilingual glossary: Categorical (Kategorikal), Numerical (Numerik), Discrete (Diskrit), Continuous (Kontinu), Composite Bar (Diagram Batang Bertumpuk).
SEN (Special Educational Needs) During PjBL Group work, assign clear, structured roles (e.g., The "Tally Master"). Reduce their dataset size to minimize arithmetic fatigue during pie chart calculations.

Appendix: PjBL Group Missions & Execution Guide

To ensure every group meets the Cambridge objectives, each group’s "Mission" requires them to collect exactly one Categorical, one Discrete, and one Continuous data set.

🟢 Group 1: The "Digital Fatigue" Analysts (Sleep & Screen Time)

Focus: Investigating the relationship between device usage and sleep quality among teenagers.

  • Categorical Question: What device do you use most in the hour before bed? (Options: Phone, Tablet, Laptop, None).
    ➔ Output: Pie Chart showing screen habits.
  • Discrete Question: How many times did you wake up in the middle of the night last night? (Counted value).
    ➔ Output: Pictogram (e.g., using a 'moon' symbol = 2 students).
  • Continuous Question: Exactly how many hours of sleep did you get last night? (e.g., 6.5 hours).
    ➔ Output: Grouped Frequency Table and an ordered Stem-and-Leaf diagram.

🔵 Group 2: The "Hydration & Fuel" Experts (Dietary Habits)

Focus: Tracking what students consume during school hours to stay energized.

  • Categorical Question: What is your favorite type of snack from the school canteen? (Options: Gorengan, Fruit, Pastry, Chips).
    ➔ Output: Bar Chart or Pie Chart.
  • Discrete Question: How many glasses/bottles of water do you drink during school hours? (Counted value).
    ➔ Output: Bar Chart.
  • Continuous Question: What is the exact volume of water (in ml) your water bottle holds? (Measured value).
    ➔ Output: Stem-and-Leaf diagram (using hundreds/tens as the stem).

🔴 Group 3: The "Active Semesta" Trackers (Physical Fitness)

Focus: Analyzing the physical activity levels of Grade 9 students.

  • Categorical Question: What is your primary method of exercise? (Options: Futsal, Basketball, Jogging, Swimming, None).
    ➔ Output: Pie Chart.
  • Discrete Question: How many days a week do you exercise for at least 30 minutes? (Counted value: 0 to 7).
    ➔ Output: Dual Bar Chart (comparing Boys vs. Girls).
  • Continuous Question: What is your resting heart rate (Beats Per Minute)? (Ask peers to find their pulse and count for 60 seconds).
    ➔ Output: Back-to-Back Stem-and-Leaf diagram.

🟡 Group 4: The "Mindful Minds" Researchers (Stress & Academics)

Focus: Understanding how students manage academic workload and stress.

  • Categorical Question: How do you usually relax after doing homework? (Options: Games, Reading, Sleeping, Music, Social Media).
    ➔ Output: Composite (Stacked) Bar Chart.
  • Discrete Question: How many extra tutoring/course sessions (les/bimbel) do you attend per week? (Counted value).
    ➔ Output: Pictogram (using a 'book' symbol).
  • Continuous Question: Exactly how much time (in minutes) did you spend on homework last night? (Measured value).
    ➔ Output: Grouped Frequency Table (e.g., 0-29 mins, 30-59 mins).

🟣 Group 5: The "Ergonomics & Commute" Investigators (Physical Posture)

Focus: Investigating physical strain on students' bodies based on how they carry things and travel.

  • Categorical Question: How do you travel to school? (Options: Car, Motorbike, Angkot, Walk).
    ➔ Output: Pie Chart.
  • Discrete Question: How many heavy textbooks are currently in your backpack? (Counted value).
    ➔ Output: Simple Bar Chart.
  • Continuous Question: What is the exact weight of your backpack today (in kg)? (Provide a digital luggage scale for the group to measure peers' bags).
    ➔ Output: Ordered Stem-and-Leaf Diagram.

💡 Teacher Tips for Execution (Meetings 1 & 2)

  1. Mission Brief Envelopes: Instead of just telling them their groups, hand each group a physical envelope labeled "TOP SECRET: DATA MISSION [Group Name]". Inside, put their 3 specific research questions. This builds the "Joyful" and "Meaningful" aspects of Deep Learning.
  2. Two-Way Table Integration: Tell all 5 groups that for their Categorical Question, they MUST record the gender of the person they are asking (Male/Female). This will force them to create a Two-Way Table (e.g., Boys who like Futsal vs Girls who like Futsal) during the Organising phase.
  3. Surveying Rules (To prevent chaos): Give the class 15-20 minutes for data collection. To ensure nobody is interviewed 5 times at once, use a "Station" format where students rotate, or assign them the homework task to survey students from other classes during break time.
