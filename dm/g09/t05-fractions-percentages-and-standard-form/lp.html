Lesson Plan: Fractions, Percentages & Standard Form

School: SMP Semesta 2026/2025 | Grade: 9

Time Allocation: 4 Meetings (2 x 40 minutes = 80 mins per meeting)

📌 Topic: 5. Fractions, percentages and standard form

Reference: Cambridge IGCSE® Math Core & Extended

Method: 5E Instructional Model (Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate)

Approach: Hands-on Activity & Deep Learning

Character Focus: Health, Self-Regulated, Collaboration, Creativity, Critical Thinking

Differentiation: Extension, Scaffolding, EAL, SEN

🎯 Objectives

  • C1.4, E1.4: Use the language and notation of proper/improper fractions and mixed numbers; Recognise equivalence and convert between forms.
  • C1.6, E1.6: Use the four operations for calculations with fractions.
  • C1.13, E1.13: Calculate a given percentage of a quantity; Express one quantity as a percentage of another; Calculate percentage increase or decrease.
  • C1.8, E1.8: Use the standard form $A \times 10^n$; Convert numbers into and out of standard form; Calculate with values in standard form.
WALT — We Are Learning To...

Learning Objective:

Apply fractions, percentages, and standard form to analyze personal and global health data: scaling nutritional recipes, calculating changes in health measurements, and comparing microscopic and macroscopic statistics.


Bloom's Level:

Understand, Apply & Analyse (Levels 2–4), with Evaluate in the reflection tasks

WILT — What I'm Looking For...

Success Criteria (self-assessable):

  1. I can convert fluently between mixed numbers and improper fractions.
  2. I can add, subtract, multiply, and divide fractions to scale recipes.
  3. I can calculate percentage increases/decreases in health metrics (e.g., heart rate, calories).
  4. I can convert microscopic sizes (viruses) and large populations into standard form ($A \times 10^n$).
  5. I can explain my method clearly to my Health Team.
🔁 Learning Model: 5E Instructional Model — Health Context "Healthy Living Analysts"
Every meeting runs a full 5E cycle. Students first meet a real health problem (Engage), investigate it with hands-on media before any rule is given (Explore), formalise the mathematics with the teacher (Explain), apply it to their Health Team's problem (Elaborate), and check their own understanding with an exit ticket and the WILT criteria (Evaluate). Students work in 4 Health Teams for the whole chapter (see Appendix).

📅 Meeting 1: Fractions — The Recipe Dilemma (Subtopics 5.1 & 5.2)

5E Phase 1 | ENGAGE — The Recipe Dilemma[10 mins]
Teacher-led & Class Discussion
  • Health Context: Show a healthy recipe for Klepon. "This serves 4 people and needs $1 \frac{1}{2}$ cups of flour. How do we make it for the whole class of 30?"
  • Students write a first guess on Worksheet 1 and share strategies. Form the 4 Health Teams (See Appendix).
5E Phase 2 | EXPLORE — Fraction Circles & Paper Folding[20 mins]
Hands-On Investigation (Pairs)
  • Joyful: Pairs use fraction circles (Hands-on 1) to try to combine $\frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{1}{3}$. They discover they must swap to sixths before counting pieces.
  • Investigate "Ali says $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2}{5}$" — use the circles to show why it is wrong.
  • Fold paper to show $\frac{2}{3}$ of $\frac{3}{4}$ and count the double-shaded boxes.
5E Phase 3 | EXPLAIN — Formalising the Four Operations[15 mins]
Direct Instruction & Guided Practice
  • Students explain their discoveries first; the teacher then names the rules: proper/improper/mixed, equivalence, Common Denominator for $+$/$-$, Top $\times$ Top & Bottom $\times$ Bottom, "Keep, Change, Flip" for $\div$.
  • Key reminder: convert mixed numbers to improper fractions first! Guided practice on Worksheet 1.
5E Phase 4 | ELABORATE — Scaling Recipes[25 mins]
Collaborative Application (Health Teams)
  • Return to the hook: scale the Klepon recipe up for 30 people and down for 3 people using a scale factor.
  • Each Health Team solves its Fractions Team Problem Card and presents one solution in 1 minute.
  • Scaffold: Provide SEN students with physical fraction bars to assist with calculations.
5E Phase 5 | EVALUATE — Exit Ticket[10 mins]
Individual Assessment
  • Exit ticket: scale $\frac{3}{4}$ cup of pandan water for 30 people. Students self-assess against WILT criteria 1 & 2.

📅 Meeting 2: Percentages in Health Data (Subtopic 5.3)

5E Phase 1 | ENGAGE — "20% Less Sugar!"[10 mins]
Visual Hook & Discussion
  • Meaningful: "A sports drink claims '20% less sugar!' If the original had 30g, how much is in the new one?" Students predict; discuss the common error $30 - 20 = 10$ g.
5E Phase 2 | EXPLORE — Nutrition Label Detectives[20 mins]
Hands-On Investigation (Pairs)
  • Shade 20% of a 100-square grid; notice that 80% is left. Pairs complete a discovery table (Original, Change, New, New ÷ Original) and spot the multiplier pattern.
  • Use the Nutrition Label Cards (Hands-on 2) to find what percentage of the daily limit each snack contains.
5E Phase 3 | EXPLAIN — The Multiplier Method[15 mins]
Direct Instruction & Guided Practice
  • Formalise: percentage of a quantity, one quantity as a percentage of another (same units), increase/decrease with multipliers ($0.80 \times 30g$ instead of subtracting).
  • Percentage change: $\frac{\text{Difference}}{\text{Original}} \times 100\%$.
5E Phase 4 | ELABORATE — Before & After Health Analysis[25 mins]
Collaborative Application (Health Teams)
  • Analyse "Before & After" health scenarios (e.g., resting heart rate drops from 85 bpm to 68 bpm after jogging for a month).
  • Each Health Team solves its Percentages Team Problem Card and explains whether the change is an increase or a decrease.
5E Phase 5 | EVALUATE — Exit Ticket[10 mins]
Individual Assessment
  • Exit ticket: sugar intake drops from 65 g to 25 g — find the percentage decrease. Self-assess WILT criterion 3.

📅 Meeting 3: The Micro and the Macro — Standard Form (Subtopic 5.4)

5E Phase 1 | ENGAGE — Counting Zeros Race[10 mins]
Visual Hook & Discussion
  • Critical Thinking: "The Covid-19 virus is $0.00000012$ m wide. Indonesia's population is $275,000,000$. How do scientists write these without making a mistake counting zeros?" Students race to read the numbers aloud.
5E Phase 2 | EXPLORE — Frog-Jump Strips[20 mins]
Hands-On Investigation (Pairs)
  • Slide a frog token (the decimal point) along digit strips (Hands-on 3) until one non-zero digit is in front. Count the jumps and record the direction.
  • Pairs find the pattern: big numbers give positive $n$, tiny numbers give negative $n$.
5E Phase 3 | EXPLAIN — $A \times 10^n$ and Index Laws[15 mins]
Direct Instruction & Guided Practice
  • Teach $A \times 10^n$, where $1 \le A < 10$. Convert into and out of standard form.
  • Multiplying standard form: $(2 \times 10^3) \times (4 \times 10^5) = 8 \times 10^8$. Use Index Laws! Adjust $A$ when it is 10 or more.
  • Scaffold: EAL students learn "Scientific Notation" as a synonym for Standard Form.
5E Phase 4 | ELABORATE — Micro → Macro Ranking[25 mins]
Collaborative Application (Health Teams)
  • Teams convert the 10 Health Data Cards (viruses, cells, populations, heartbeats) into standard form and rank them from smallest to largest.
  • Each Health Team solves its Standard Form Team Problem Card.
5E Phase 5 | EVALUATE — Exit Ticket & Reflection[10 mins]
Individual Assessment
  • Exit ticket: $(3.7 \times 10^{13}) \times 30$ in standard form. Reflection: "How does writing numbers in standard form prevent dangerous medical/scientific errors?"

📅 Meeting 4: Chapter Review & Summative Evaluation

Review | "I Have… Who Has…?" Loop Game[20 mins]
Whole-Class Game
  • Joyful: Each student holds a loop card (Hands-on 4) mixing fractions, percentages and standard form. The chain must return to the start card — time the class and try to beat the record.
  • Teacher notes the cards that cause hesitation and re-explains them briefly.
5E Phase 5 | EVALUATE — Chapter 5 Test[60 mins]
Summative Test

Chapter 5 Evaluation (55 min):

  • Remove visual aids. Distribute the Chapter 5 Test (Worksheet 4) covering mixed number arithmetic, percentage changes, and standard form operations.

Closure & Reflection (5 min):

  • Self-Reflection: students rate themselves on all 5 WILT criteria and set one goal for the next chapter.
🌟 Differentiation Strategies
Profile Strategies & Actions in Class
Extension (High Achievers) Challenge with complex fraction division involving algebraic variables. Provide standard form addition/subtraction problems (which require making indices match first before adding the mantissa). Ask them to calculate compound percentage changes (e.g., population growth over 5 years).
Scaffolding (Middle/Low) Provide fraction bars/circles to physically verify arithmetic during worksheet time. Give a "Percentage Multiplier Translation Sheet" (e.g., 20% discount $\rightarrow$ Multiply by 0.8).
EAL (English Additional Language) Provide a bilingual glossary: Numerator (Pembilang), Denominator (Penyebut), Proper Fraction (Pecahan Biasa), Mixed Number (Pecahan Campuran). Emphasize visual steps over dense word problems.
SEN (Special Educational Needs) Draw visual "loops" or "frog jumps" physically on paper when converting decimals to Standard Form. Highlight the target integer ($A$) in a bright color to ensure they place the decimal correctly.

Appendix: Health Team Problems (Elaborate Phase)

Students stay in the same Health Team for the whole chapter. In the Elaborate phase of each meeting, the team solves its problem for that meeting's subtopic and presents it to the class.

🟢 Team 1: The Dietary Planners (Nutrition Focus)

  • Meeting 1 — Fractions: Scale a healthy salad recipe (using mixed numbers like $1 \frac{3}{4}$ cups of spinach) originally for 4 people up to feed 18 people.
  • Meeting 2 — Percentages: A student replaces sugary soda with water. Their daily sugar intake drops from 65g to 25g. Calculate the exact percentage decrease.
  • Meeting 3 — Standard Form: A Vitamin C molecule is about $0.0000000007$ m across. Write it in Standard Form and compare it with a red blood cell ($0.000008$ m).

🔵 Team 2: The Epidemiologists (Disease Focus)

  • Meeting 1 — Fractions: A hospital has 480 beds and $\frac{5}{8}$ of them are occupied. If they add 120 new beds, what fraction is now occupied?
  • Meeting 2 — Percentages: A vaccination campaign reduces flu cases from 4,500 to 1,200 in one month. Calculate the percentage decrease.
  • Meeting 3 — Standard Form: Write the diameter of the Influenza virus ($0.0000001$ meters) and the global population ($8,100,000,000$) in Standard Form. Calculate how many viruses laid end-to-end span the equator ($4 \times 10^7$ m).

🔴 Team 3: The Fitness Coaches (Exercise Focus)

  • Meeting 1 — Fractions: A runner completes $3 \frac{1}{2}$ laps of a track. Each lap is $\frac{2}{5}$ of a kilometer. Calculate the total distance run using fraction multiplication.
  • Meeting 2 — Percentages: An athlete's resting heart rate improves from 78 bpm to 62 bpm after a training camp. Express the improvement as a percentage decrease.
  • Meeting 3 — Standard Form: An average human heart beats about $35,000,000$ times a year. Write this in Standard Form. Calculate how many times it beats in an 80-year lifespan, leaving the answer in Standard Form.

🟡 Team 4: The Hydration Specialists (Water Focus)

  • Meeting 1 — Fractions: A water dispenser holds $15 \frac{1}{2}$ liters. If a student's bottle holds $\frac{3}{4}$ of a liter, how many full bottles can be filled from the dispenser?
  • Meeting 2 — Percentages: A student aims to drink 2.5 liters of water a day. By noon, they have drunk 800 ml. What percentage of their daily goal have they reached?
  • Meeting 3 — Standard Form: The human body contains approximately $3.7 \times 10^{13}$ cells, and each cell is mostly water. Calculate the total number of cells in a classroom of 30 students using Standard Form multiplication.

💡 Teacher Tips for Execution

  1. Explore before Explain: Resist giving the rule during Explore. Let pairs struggle with the fraction circles, 100-grids and frog strips first; the Explain phase then starts from their words.
  2. Fraction Manipulatives (Meeting 1): Use physical plastic fraction circles (or the paper cut-outs) when reviewing addition/subtraction to ensure students visually remember why finding a Common Denominator is mandatory.
  3. The "Jump" Method (Meeting 3): When teaching Standard Form, explicitly draw the "frog jumps" under the zeros on the whiteboard to help SEN and visual learners count the index $n$ accurately.
  4. Printing: Every worksheet and hands-on file is laid out as full A4 pages — one file per meeting — so print each one double-sided with no wasted paper.
