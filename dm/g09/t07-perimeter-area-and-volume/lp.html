Lesson Plan: Perimeter, Area and Volume

School: SMP Semesta 2026/2025 | Grade: 9

Time Allocation: 6 Meetings (2 x 40 minutes = 80 mins per meeting)

📌 Topic: 7. Perimeter, area and volume

Reference: Cambridge IGCSE® Math Core & Extended

Method: Discovery Learning

Approach: Deep Learning & Hands-on Activity

Character Focus: Self-Regulated, Creativity, Critical Thinking

Differentiation: Extension, Scaffolding, EAL, SEN

🎯 Objectives

  • C5.2, E5.2: Carry out calculations involving the perimeter and area of a rectangle, triangle, parallelogram and trapezium.
  • C5.4, E5.4: Carry out calculations involving the surface area and volume of a cuboid, prism, cylinder, sphere, pyramid, cone.
WALT — We Are Learning To...

Learning Objective:

Discover the mathematical formulas for 2D areas and 3D volumes/surface areas through physical manipulation, and apply these formulas to solve complex, real-world geometric problems.


Bloom's Level:

Apply, Analyse & Evaluate (Levels 3–5)

WILT — What I'm Looking For...

Success Criteria (self-assessable):

  1. I can differentiate between 1D (perimeter), 2D (area), and 3D (volume).
  2. I can calculate the area of composite shapes by breaking them down into rectangles, triangles, or trapeziums.
  3. I can visualize and draw 2D nets for 3D solids to calculate surface area.
  4. I can calculate the volume of prisms and cylinders ($V = A \times h$) and pyramids/cones ($V = \frac{1}{3} A \times h$).
🧰 Materials for every meeting
Each meeting has a Worksheet and a Hands-on Activity (full A4 pages), plus an Interactive Media file that is the digital twin of that Hands-on Activity — use it on the projector or student devices when there is no time to print. Students work individually throughout.

📅 Meeting 1: Perimeter and Basic 2D Area (Subtopic 7.1)

Phase 1 & 2 | Stimulation & Problem Statement[20 mins]
Teacher-led & Class Discussion

Stimulation (10 min):

  • Meaningful: Display an image of a rice paddy (Sawah). "A farmer wants to put a fence around the Sawah to keep animals out, and buy fertilizer to cover the inside soil. What is the difference between these two mathematical measurements?"

Problem Statement (10 min):

  • How can we prove the area formula for a Parallelogram and Triangle if we only know the formula for a Rectangle ($L \times W$)?
Phase 3 & 4 | Data Collection & Processing[30 mins]
Individual Hands-On Activity

Paper Cutting Discovery (30 min):

  • Joyful: Give each student the paper parallelograms and triangles on 1 cm grids (Hands-on 1).
  • Instruct them to make exactly ONE cut with scissors to transform the parallelogram into a perfect rectangle. (They will cut off a right triangle and move it to the other side).
  • Critical Thinking: Students deduce that Area of Parallelogram = Base $\times$ Perpendicular Height.
  • Repeat for triangles (two identical triangles form a parallelogram/rectangle, hence Area = $\frac{1}{2} b \times h$).
  • No time to print? Interactive Media 1 "Cut & Slide" lets students make the cut and slide the pieces on screen.
Phase 5 & 6 | Verification & Generalization[30 mins]
Independent Practice

Verification & Practice (30 min):

  • Students complete Worksheet 1, practicing perimeter and area of basic shapes.
  • Generalization/Exit Ticket: Why must we use the perpendicular height, and not the slant length, for area?

📅 Meeting 2: Trapeziums and Composite Shapes (Subtopic 7.1)

Phase 1 & 2 | Stimulation & Problem Statement[20 mins]
Visual Concept Building

Stimulation (10 min):

  • Show an irregular, L-shaped room. "If we want to buy carpet for this room, there is no single formula. What can we do?" (Break it down!).

Problem Statement (10 min):

  • "What about a Trapezium? It has two parallel sides, but they are different lengths. How do we find its area?"
Phase 3 & 4 | Data Collection & Processing[25 mins]
Guided Discovery (Individual)

Discovering the Trapezium Formula (25 min):

  • Each student uses the trapezium on grid paper (Hands-on 2) and draws a diagonal line cutting it into TWO triangles.
  • Self-Regulated: Students realize one triangle's base is '$a$' and the other's base is '$b$'. Both share height '$h$'.
  • They process the formula: $\frac{1}{2}ah + \frac{1}{2}bh = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
  • No time to print? Interactive Media 2 "Trapezium & Composite Lab" shows the diagonal split and the split-or-subtract methods for L-shaped rooms.
Phase 5 & 6 | Verification & Generalization[35 mins]
Independent Practice

Verification & Practice (35 min):

  • Worksheet 2: Calculating area of trapeziums and complex composite shapes (adding and subtracting areas).
  • Scaffold: Provide EAL/SEN students with dotted lines showing exactly where to split composite shapes into simpler rectangles.
  • Generalization/Exit Ticket: Find the area of a trapezium with parallel sides 5 cm and 9 cm and height 6 cm.

📅 Meeting 3: 3D Objects and Nets (Subtopic 7.2)

Phase 1 & 2 | Stimulation & Problem Statement[20 mins]
Hands-On Investigation

Stimulation (10 min):

  • Hold up an empty cereal box (Cuboid) and a Toblerone box (Triangular Prism).
  • "A 3D object takes up space. But its outside 'skin' is completely flat. How can we flatten these objects?"

Problem Statement (10 min):

  • How do we accurately draw a 2D map (a 'net') that can fold perfectly into a 3D solid?
Phase 3 & 4 | Data Collection & Processing[30 mins]
Motoric Activity (Individual)

Deconstructing Solids (30 min):

  • Joyful: Each student is given a small cardboard packaging box. They must physically cut along the edges to unfold it into a single, flat 2D net.
  • Students trace their nets onto A3 paper and label the dimensions (Length, Width, Height) on the corresponding 2D flaps. Printed nets to cut and fold (Hands-on 3) are provided for anyone without a box.
  • No time to print? Interactive Media 3 "Net Folder" folds and unfolds the nets of a cuboid, triangular prism, cylinder and square pyramid in 3D.
Phase 5 & 6 | Verification & Generalization[30 mins]
Direct Instruction & Practice

Generalization (30 min):

  • Teacher formalizes the definition of Prisms (uniform cross-section).
  • Worksheet 3: Visualizing and drawing nets for cylinders (a rectangle wrapping around two circles) and pyramids.
  • Exit Ticket: If a cylinder has height $h$ and radius $r$, what is the length of the rectangular part of its net? (Answer: $2\pi r$).
  • Teacher key — Worksheet 3 "nets of a cube": A ✅, B ❌, C ✅, D ❌, E ✅, F ❌ (students can check by folding them in Interactive Media 3, Tab 2).

📅 Meeting 4: Surface Area of Solids (Subtopic 7.3)

Phase 1 & 2 | Stimulation & Problem Statement[15 mins]
Contextual Application

Stimulation (15 min):

  • Meaningful: "You are hired to wrap a gift box. Wrapping paper costs Rp 5,000 per square meter. How do you calculate exactly how much paper you need without wasting any?"
  • Problem: How do we translate our 2D nets into a total Surface Area calculation?
Phase 3 & 4 | Data Collection & Processing[30 mins]
Individual Calculation

Calculating the Nets (30 min):

  • Students return to the nets they traced in Meeting 3 (or the measured nets in Hands-on 4). They measure the exact dimensions with rulers.
  • Critical Thinking: Students calculate the area of each individual polygon on their net, then sum them up to find the Total Surface Area (TSA).
  • Teacher derives the cylinder TSA formula on the board: $2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh$.
  • No time to print? Interactive Media 4 "Gift Wrap Calculator" unfolds each solid and adds up its face areas live.
Phase 5 & 6 | Verification & Generalization[35 mins]
Independent Practice

Elaborate (35 min):

  • Worksheet 4: Calculating Surface Area of cuboids, triangular prisms, and cylinders.
  • Scaffold: Students list every face in a table (shape, dimensions, area, how many) before adding up.
  • Extension: Students calculate the surface area of a composite solid (e.g., a cylinder glued on top of a cuboid - remembering to subtract the hidden overlapping area!).
  • Generalization/Exit Ticket: Find the total surface area of a cylinder with radius 5 cm and height 10 cm.

📅 Meeting 5: Volume of Solids (Subtopic 7.3)

Phase 1 & 2 | Stimulation & Problem Statement[15 mins]
Teacher-led Exploration

Stimulation (15 min):

  • "Surface area is the 'skin'. Volume is the 'meat'. How much space is inside?"
  • Show a prism. Explain that a prism is just its base shape stacked repeatedly to a certain height. Hence, $V = \text{Area of Base} \times \text{Height}$.
Phase 3 & 4 | Data Collection & Processing[30 mins]
Hands-On Discovery (Individual)

The "Rice Pouring" Experiment (30 min):

  • Joyful: Each student builds a hollow square prism and a hollow square pyramid with the exact same base and height from the nets in Hands-on 5 (base 5 cm, height 6 cm).
  • Task: Fill the pyramid with rice (or water) and pour it into the prism. How many pyramids does it take to fill the prism perfectly?
  • Students discover the relationship: It takes exactly 3! Therefore, Volume of a Pyramid/Cone = $\frac{1}{3} \times \text{Base Area} \times \text{Height}$.
  • No time to print? Interactive Media 5 "Rice Pouring Lab" pours virtual rice from pyramids and cones into prisms and cylinders.
Phase 5 & 6 | Verification & Generalization[35 mins]
Independent Practice

Elaborate (35 min):

  • Worksheet 5: Calculating volumes of cylinders, prisms, cones, and pyramids.
  • Exit Ticket: A cone and a cylinder have the same radius and height. The cylinder's volume is 300 cm³. What is the cone's volume?

📅 Meeting 6: Spheres & Chapter Evaluation (Subtopic 7.3)

Phase 1-3 | Spheres & Final Synthesis[25 mins]
Concept & Demonstration

Hook — Peeling the Orange (15 min):

  • Show a sphere (an orange). Draw 4 circles on paper using the orange's diameter (template in Hands-on 6).
  • Peel the orange and show that the peel perfectly fills exactly 4 flat circles.
  • Generalize: Surface Area of Sphere = $4 \pi r^2$. Volume = $\frac{4}{3} \pi r^3$.
  • No time to print or no oranges? Interactive Media 6 "Orange Peel & Review" peels a virtual orange into 4 circles and runs the review games.

Synthesis (10 min):

  • Quick whiteboard review distinguishing 1D (Perimeter), 2D (Area), and 3D (Volume) units, then the "I have… who has…?" review loop (Hands-on 6).
Phase 4-6 | Summative Evaluation[55 mins]
Summative Test

Chapter 7 Evaluation (45 min):

  • Remove visual aids. Distribute the Chapter Test (Worksheet 6, password-protected) covering composite areas, working backwards from volume, and calculating surface areas from nets.
  • Self-Regulated: Students manage their time through complex multi-step geometry problems.

Closure & Reflection (10 min):

  • Self-Reflection: "Which formula did you find most difficult to memorize? Did the physical experiments (rice/paper cutting) help you remember them?"
🌟 Differentiation Strategies
Profile Strategies & Actions in Class
Extension (High Achievers) Challenge with "working backwards" problems (e.g., given the volume of a cylinder is $500\pi$ and height is $20$, find the radius). Introduce calculating the volume and surface area of a Frustum (a cone with the top cut off) using similar triangles.
Scaffolding (Middle/Low) Provide a physical "Formula Cheat Sheet" for them to refer to during exercises. Provide composite shapes with dotted lines already drawn in to show exactly where to split the shape into rectangles/triangles.
EAL (English Additional Language) Provide a visual glossary: Perimeter (Keliling), Area (Luas), Volume (Volume), Surface Area (Luas Permukaan), Prism (Prisma), Pyramid (Limas). Emphasize matching the English word to a 3D picture.
SEN (Special Educational Needs) During the 3D Net activity, provide pre-cut nets with fold lines clearly marked so they only need to focus on folding and measuring, reducing fine-motor strain. The Interactive Media versions of each activity can replace cutting entirely.
