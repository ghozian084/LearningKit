Lesson Plan: 8. Introduction to Probability

School: SMP Semesta 2026/2025 | Grade: 9

Time Allocation: 6 Meetings (2 x 40 minutes = 80 mins per meeting)

📌 Topic: 8. Introduction to probability (pp. 228–245)

Reference: Cambridge IGCSE® Mathematics Core & Extended

Method: Discovery Learning

Approach: Realistic Mathematics Education (RME)

Character Focus: Self-Regulated, Creativity, Critical Thinking

Differentiation: Extension, Scaffolding, EAL, SEN

🎯 Objectives

  • C8.1, E8.1: Understand and use the probability scale from 0 to 1; calculate the probability of a single event, and use $P(A') = 1 - P(A)$.
  • C8.2, E8.2: Understand relative frequency (experimental probability) as an estimate of probability, and calculate expected frequencies.
  • C8.3, E8.3: Calculate the probability of combined events using sample space diagrams.
  • C8.3, E8.3: Identify and calculate probabilities of combined independent and mutually exclusive events.
WALT — We Are Learning To...

Learning Objective:

Quantify the likelihood of everyday teen-relevant events on a scale from 0 to 1, investigate experimental vs. theoretical probability, construct sample space diagrams for combined events, and distinguish between independent and mutually exclusive events.


Bloom's Level:

Understand, Apply & Analyse (Levels 2–4)

WILT — What I'm Looking For...

Success Criteria (self-assessable):

  1. I can find experimental probability (relative frequency) from trials and estimate expected occurrences.
  2. I can calculate theoretical probability as a simplified fraction, decimal, or percentage.
  3. I can find the probability that an event does NOT happen using $P(A') = 1 - P(A)$.
  4. I can draw a Sample Space Diagram to list all outcomes of two combined events.
  5. I know when to ADD probabilities (Mutually Exclusive / OR) and when to MULTIPLY them (Independent / AND).
🧰 Materials for every meeting
Each meeting has a Worksheet and a Hands-on Activity (full A4 pages), plus an Interactive Media file that is the digital twin of that Hands-on Activity — use it on the projector or student devices when there is no time to print. Board 1 follows this plan slide by slide. Students work individually throughout; experiment results are pooled as class data on the board. Worksheet 6 is the password-protected Chapter 8 Test (with answer key).

📅 Meeting 1: Probability Scale, Gacha Drop Rates & Trials (Subtopic 8.1, pp. 229–231)

Phase 1 & 2 | Stimulation & Problem Statement[25 mins]
Teen RME Hook &
Calculator Game

Stimulation — Video Game "Gacha" & Higher or Lower (15 min):

  • RME Context: "In mobile games, a Rare Mystery Box has a $\frac{1}{4}$ (25%) drop rate for a legendary skin (just like the $\frac{1}{4}$ chance in p. 229!). Ask: "If you open 4 boxes, does that GUARANTEE you get 3 common items and 1 legendary item? Why or why not?""
  • Play the book's Getting Started: Higher or Lower game (p. 229) using the calculator's random number button (ran / ran#) generating decimals between 0 and 1.

Problem Statement (10 min):

  • Critical Thinking: "How can you maximize your winning streak in Higher or Lower? For which numbers do you guess 'higher' vs 'lower'?" Introduce the Probability Scale (0 to 1) and key words: trial, outcome, event, sample space.
  • No time to print? Interactive Media 1 "Trial Lab" plays Higher or Lower on screen and has the drag-and-drop probability scale.
Phase 3 & 4 | Data Collection & Processing[30 mins]
Experimental Activity
(Individual)

Basketball Free-Throws / Paper Toss & Relative Frequency (30 min):

  • Relate to Worked Example 1 (p. 230): Instead of just blindfolded darts, frame it as a classroom paper-ball trash-bin toss (or archery/darts): 15 hits in 125 throws $= \frac{15}{125} = 0.12$.
  • Each student does a 25-trial experiment (tossing a crumpled paper ball into a bin or flipping a plastic bottle cap) and tallies their successes on Hands-on 1.
  • Pool every student's data (20 students × 25 = 500 trials) on the board. Calculate Experimental Probability (Relative Frequency) $= \frac{\text{number of successes}}{\text{number of trials}}$.
  • Discover the rule: The more trials you run, the more reliable your probability estimate becomes.
  • No time to print or no space to throw? Interactive Media 1 runs 1, 25 or 500 virtual bottle-cap flips and plots the relative frequency settling down.
Phase 5 & 6 | Verification & Generalization[25 mins]
Independent Practice

Expected Occurrences & Practice (25 min):

  • Introduce Expected Occurrences (pp. 230–231): If your winning drop rate in a game is 18%, how many wins do you expect in 80 plays ($14.4 \approx 14$) or 200 plays ($36$)?
  • Students solve Exercise 8.1 Q1–Q3 (p. 233–234: 100 dice throws, 8-sector spinner, solar lamp lifetimes) and Worksheet 1.
  • Scaffold: Remind students that probability can be written as a simplified fraction, decimal, or percentage, but fractions are best when decimals repeat.

📅 Meeting 2: Theoretical Probability, Complements & Myth-Busting (Subtopic 8.1, pp. 231–235)

Phase 1 & 2 | Stimulation & Problem Statement[20 mins]
Teen RME Hook &
Concept Shift

Stimulation (10 min):

  • RME Context (p. 232): Frame Worked Example 4 as "Running late for school and grabbing socks in the dark!" (Nasir has 20 socks: 8 red, 10 blue, 2 green). Frame Worked Example 5 as "9 teams at School Sports Day randomly drawing one letter from the word HOLLYWOOD (or SEMESTA99) as their team badge."

Problem Statement (10 min):

  • "When all items are equally sized (unbiased), do we need to run 100 experiments? How do we quickly find $P(Y)$, $P(O)$, $P(H \text{ or } L)$, and $P(Z)$?"
  • Formalize Theoretical Probability $= \frac{\text{favourable outcomes}}{\text{possible outcomes}}$ and discuss biased vs unbiased (fair) objects.
Phase 3 & 4 | Data Collection & Investigation[30 mins]
Individual Investigation
& Class Debate

Complementary Events & TikTok/Social Media Myth-Busting (30 min):

  • Introduce Worked Example 6 (p. 233): Jas's driving test ($P(\text{pass}) = \frac{2}{3} \implies P(\text{fails}) = 1 - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{3}$). Generalize $P(A') = 1 - P(A)$ ("not A", like $P(\text{No Rain}) = 1 - P(\text{Rain})$ before hanging out on Saturday).
  • Book Investigation — "Talking about probability" (p. 233) as Myth-Busting: Each student judges the viral "bad math" claims on the Hands-on 2 myth cards (BUSTED or TRUE, with a reason), then the class debates: e.g., "On a 10-question True/False quiz, guessing guarantees 5 right", "In a match you win, lose, or draw, so your chance of winning is always $\frac{1}{3}$", "Rolling four 6s in 10 rolls proves the die is rigged!"
  • No time to print? Interactive Media 2 "Myth Lab" draws letter tiles on screen and simulates each myth (e.g. guessing 10 True/False questions 1000 times: exactly 5 right only about 25% of the time).
Phase 5 & 6 | Verification & Exit Ticket[30 mins]
Practice & Self-Check

Practice & Textbook Exit Ticket (30 min):

  • Students complete Exercise 8.1 Q4–Q9 (p. 234–235: favourite film genres bar chart, flowers, marbles in a bag, 52-card pack) and Worksheet 2.
  • Exit Ticket (p. 235 Self Assessment): Complete the 4-event Exit Ticket table (Event A: >2 on die; Event B: red card from 12 red, 12 white, 6 blue; Event C: rolling a 7; Event D: 14 heads in 20 tosses) + choose an emoji reflection!
  • Scaffold: Provide EAL/unfamiliar students with the textbook Tip on p. 232 explaining the 4 suits (hearts, diamonds, clubs, spades) and 13 cards per suit.

📅 Meeting 3: Sample Space Diagrams — School Bazaar Booth (Subtopic 8.2, pp. 235–238)

Phase 1 & 2 | Stimulation & Problem Statement[20 mins]
RME Game Context

Stimulation — The School Bazaar Dice Booth (10 min):

  • RME Context (p. 236, WE 7): "At the SMP Semesta School Bazaar, you run a game booth. Players roll a Red die and a Blue die and ADD the numbers. They win a free boba if they roll a sum of 7, or lose if they roll less than 5."

Problem Statement (10 min):

  • "Sums go from 2 to 12 (11 different totals). Does that mean getting a 7 has the same $\frac{1}{11}$ chance as getting a 2? How do we list ALL combinations fairly so we don't go bankrupt at the bazaar?"
Phase 3 & 4 | Data Collection & Processing[35 mins]
Individual Discovery

Constructing Sample Space Diagrams (35 min):

  • Each student rolls two dice 25 times and tallies the sums on Hands-on 3, noticing 7 keeps coming up way more often than 2 or 12! (Pool the class tallies on the board.)
  • Guide students to build the $6 \times 6$ Sample Space Diagram (p. 236) showing all 36 equally likely outcomes.
  • Verify Worked Example 7 results: $P(7) = \frac{6}{36} = \frac{1}{6}$, $P(< 5) = \frac{6}{36} = \frac{1}{6}$, $P(\ge 8) = \frac{15}{36} = \frac{5}{12}$, and use complements for $P(< 8) = 1 - \frac{5}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$.
  • Creativity: Solve Exercise 8.2 Q5 (p. 238)—act as detectives comparing Set A (4-sided + 8-sided dice) vs Set B (two 6-sided dice) against experimental frequencies to catch which set was actually used! (Hands-on 3, page 2 has a second detective case.)
  • No time to print or no dice? Interactive Media 3 "Bazaar Booth" rolls two dice, builds the 6 × 6 grid (sum, product, difference or HCF) and runs the detective case.
Phase 5 & 6 | Verification & Peer Assessment[25 mins]
Practice & Peer Review

Practice & Peer Checklist (25 min):

  • Students complete Exercise 8.2 Q1–Q4 (p. 236–237: coin tossed twice, product of two dice, 5-sector spinner + tetrahedral die recording the greater number, HCF of two dice) and Worksheet 3.
  • Peer Assessment (p. 238): Students swap sample space diagrams with the person next to them and verify: Are all possible outcomes indicated? Is it easy to read?
  • Scaffold: Provide SEN students with pre-drawn empty $6 \times 6$ grids and $5 \times 4$ grids so they focus purely on computing sums/products/HCFs.

📅 Meeting 4: Independent Events — The "AND" Rule (Subtopic 8.3, p. 239)

Phase 1 & 2 | Stimulation & Problem Statement[20 mins]
Teen RME Context

Stimulation — Two Friends, Two Separate Challenges (10 min):

  • RME Context (p. 239–240): "Suppose you flip a coin twice, or draw a mystery candy from a jar, look at the flavor, and put it back before your friend draws." Also connect to Worked Example 8: "Noa and Sunja are taking their music/coding exam in separate rooms. Does Noa's score affect Sunja's score? No—they are Independent Events!"

Problem Statement (10 min):

  • "If Noa has a $\frac{3}{4}$ chance to pass AND Sunja has a $\frac{5}{6}$ chance to pass, what is the chance BOTH pass? Why can't we add $\frac{3}{4} + \frac{5}{6}$?" (Because it would be greater than 1!).
Phase 3 & 4 | Data Collection & Processing[30 mins]
Guided Discovery

Deriving the Multiplication Rule (30 min):

  • Check with the coin flip sample space (HH, HT, TH, TT): $P(\text{H and H}) = \frac{1}{4}$. Notice $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{4}$!
  • Each student spins the Noa spinner ($\frac34$ PASS) and the Sunja spinner ($\frac56$ PASS) from Hands-on 4 24 times, then shades the $4 \times 6$ area model: 15 of the 24 cells are "both pass".
  • Generalize: For independent events, $P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \times P(B)$. (Trigger word: AND = Multiply).
  • Solve Worked Example 8a & 8b (p. 240): $P(\text{both pass}) = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{5}{6} = \frac{5}{8}$, and $P(\text{neither passes}) = \frac{1}{4} \times \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{24}$.
  • No time to print? Interactive Media 4 "Two Spinners" spins both spinners (1, 24 or 1000 times) and shades the area model for any two probabilities.
Phase 5 & 6 | Verification & Practice[30 mins]
Independent Practice

Elaborate (30 min):

  • Students practice Exercise 8.3 Q1, Q2 (i–iii), & Q4 (a–c) (p. 242: dice thrown twice, 12 coloured balls drawn with replacement, Kirti & Hussein driving test) and Worksheet 4.
  • Self-Regulated: Check that every "AND" probability is smaller than each of the individual probabilities and stays between 0 and 1.

📅 Meeting 5: Mutually Exclusive Events — The "OR" Rule (Subtopic 8.3, pp. 240–242)

Phase 1 & 2 | Stimulation & Problem Statement[20 mins]
Teen RME Discussion

Stimulation — School Club Schedules & Overlapping Traits (10 min):

  • RME Context (p. 240): "At SMP Semesta, Futsal Club and Robotics Club both meet on Tuesday at 3:00 PM. Can you attend BOTH at the exact same time? No—they are Mutually Exclusive! Now compare: Can you 'own a smartphone' AND 'wear glasses' at the same time? Yes—those overlap (NOT mutually exclusive), just like rolling an 'even number' and 'less than 4' on a die (2 fits both!)."

Problem Statement (10 min):

  • "When events CANNOT happen together (like passing and failing the same test), how do we find $P(A \text{ or } B)$?"
Phase 3 & 4 | Data Collection & Processing[30 mins]
Combined Problem Solving

Combining AND & OR Rules (30 min):

  • Each student sorts the Hands-on 5 scenario cards into "can happen together" and "cannot happen together (mutually exclusive)", and tests the die events on the Venn mat.
  • Generalize: For mutually exclusive events, $P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B)$. (Trigger word: OR = Add).
  • Walk through Worked Example 8c & 8d (p. 241):
    c) $P(\text{at least one passes}) = 1 - P(\text{neither passes}) = 1 - \frac{1}{24} = \frac{23}{24}$.
    d) $P(\text{either Noa or Sunja passes, not both})$ = $P(\text{Noa passes AND Sunja fails})$ OR $P(\text{Noa fails AND Sunja passes}) = \frac{3}{24} + \frac{5}{24} = \frac{1}{3}$.
  • Walk through Worked Example 9 (p. 241): Simone (0.1) and Rami (0.2) shooting basketballs into the net. Find $P(\text{exactly one scores}) = 0.1 \times 0.8 + 0.9 \times 0.2 = 0.26$.
  • No time to print? Interactive Media 5 "OR Lab" sorts the scenario cards, tests any two die events on a Venn diagram, and shades "exactly one / at least one" on a 10 × 10 grid.
Phase 5 & 6 | Verification & Practice[30 mins]
Independent Practice

Elaborate & Discussion (30 min):

  • Think – share (p. 242): each student writes 3 everyday teen examples of mutually exclusive events (e.g., ordering Iced Tea vs Hot Tea for a single drink, or getting an A vs getting a C on one report card), then a few are shared with the class.
  • Students complete the rest of Exercise 8.3 (Q2 b iv–vii, Q3, Q4 d–e) (Devin & Tej playing cards with replacement, Kirti & Hussein "at least one" / "exactly one") and Worksheet 5.

📅 Meeting 6: Spin-the-Wheel Synthesis & Summative Evaluation (pp. 243–245)

Phase 1-4 | Spin-the-Wheel Food Stall Synthesis[25 mins]
Concept Consolidation

Exam-Style Synthesis (25 min):

  • Review the Chapter Summary checklist (p. 243).
  • RME Context — Practice Question 6 (pp. 244–245): Frame as a "Spin-the-Wheel Food Stall" prize game using a Square spinner (1–4) and a Pentagon spinner (1–5) (printable spinners on Hands-on 6). Event $A$ wins a snack if the total is 5. Event $B$ wins a drink if the difference is 1.
  • Creativity & Critical Thinking: Students use their sample space to prove why $P(A \text{ or } B) \neq P(A) + P(B)$ here (because outcomes $(2,3)$ and $(3,2)$ win BOTH prizes—they are not mutually exclusive!): $P(A) = \frac{4}{20}$, $P(B) = \frac{7}{20}$, but $P(A \text{ or } B) = \frac{9}{20}$.
  • No time to print? Interactive Media 6 "Food Stall" spins both spinners and highlights A, B, "A and B" and "A or B" on the 4 × 5 grid; Tab 2 runs the review loop.
Phase 5 & 6 | Summative Evaluation & Self-Assessment[55 mins]
Summative Test

Chapter 8 Evaluation (45 min):

  • Distribute the Chapter 8 Test (Worksheet 6, password-protected) in the style of Practice Questions 1–5 (pp. 243–244: fruit bowl, quadrilaterals, biased 4-sided and 6-sided dice, coins sample space).

Closure — Textbook Self-Assessment (10 min):

  • Using the headings from p. 245, students reflect in their notebooks: My strengths, Evidence of my strengths, My goals, My plan to reach the goals.

🔑 Teacher Key — Lesson Examples

  • M1: $\frac{15}{125} = 0.12$ · 18% of 80 plays $= 14.4 \approx 14$; of 200 plays $= 36$ · Higher or Lower: guess "higher" when the number is below 0.5.
  • M2: Socks: $P(\text{red}) = \frac{8}{20} = \frac25$ · HOLLYWOOD: $P(Y) = \frac19$, $P(O) = \frac39 = \frac13$, $P(H \text{ or } L) = \frac39 = \frac13$, $P(Z) = 0$ · Myths: exactly 5 of 10 by guessing ≈ 0.246; four or more 6s in 10 rolls of a fair die ≈ 0.070 (happens about 1 time in 14) · Exit ticket: A $\frac23$, B $\frac{12}{30} = \frac25$, C $0$, D $\frac{14}{20} = 0.7$ (experimental).
  • M3: $P(7) = \frac16$, $P(<5) = \frac16$, $P(\ge 8) = \frac{5}{12}$, $P(<8) = \frac{7}{12}$ · Set A (4-sided + 8-sided) sums 2–12 occur 1, 2, 3, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 3, 2, 1 times out of 32: a flat top from 5 to 9 (Set B peaks at 7).
  • M4: $\frac34 \times \frac56 = \frac{15}{24} = \frac58$ · $\frac14 \times \frac16 = \frac{1}{24}$.
  • M5: at least one $= \frac{23}{24}$ · exactly one $= \frac{3}{24} + \frac{5}{24} = \frac13$ · Simone/Rami exactly one $= 0.26$.
  • M6: $P(A) = \frac{4}{20} = \frac15$, $P(B) = \frac{7}{20}$, $P(A \text{ and } B) = \frac{2}{20}$, $P(A \text{ or } B) = \frac{9}{20}$.
🌟 Differentiation Strategies
Profile Strategies & Actions in Class
Extension (High Achievers) Assign Practice Question 5 & 6 (pp. 244–245) involving biased 6-sided dice with unequal probabilities and overlapping combined events ($P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \text{ and } B)$). Challenge them to compare probabilities with vs. without replacement (previewing Ch. 24).
Scaffolding (Middle/Low) Provide a visual "Trigger Word" anchor chart: OR = ADD (+) (for mutually exclusive), AND = MULTIPLY ($\times$) (for independent), and NOT = $1 - P(A)$. Provide pre-drawn empty sample space grids for Ex 8.2 Q1–Q4.
EAL (English Additional Language) Provide a bilingual glossary for the book's Key Words: Bias (Bias/Tidak adil), Trial (Percobaan), Outcome (Hasil), Event (Kejadian), Sample Space (Ruang Sampel), Mutually Exclusive (Saling Lepas), Independent (Saling Bebas), Relative Frequency (Frekuensi Relatif). Explicitly explain playing card terms (suits/ranks) and terms like "at least" vs "exactly one".
SEN (Special Educational Needs) Provide physical 6-sided dice, tetrahedral dice, coins, and colored counters during exploratory phases. Allow leaving answers as unsimplified fractions initially (e.g., $\frac{6}{36}$) to verify the counting logic before simplifying, and use color-coded grid overlays for sample space problems.
