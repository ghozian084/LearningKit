The Problem

The tide height at Tanjung Priok port is

$h(t) = 1.2 + 0.6\sin(30t)^\circ + 0.8\cos(30t)^\circ$ m

($t$ = hours after midnight). A cargo ship needs $h \ge 1.8$ m to dock.

When is it safe to dock? What is the highest tide?

Meeting 1 · Explain sec, cosec, cot

Definitions

$\sec\theta = \dfrac{1}{\cos\theta}$   $\operatorname{cosec}\theta = \dfrac{1}{\sin\theta}$   $\cot\theta = \dfrac{1}{\tan\theta} = \dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}$

3rd letter → parent: sec ↔ cos, cosec ↔ sin

Identities divide $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta \equiv 1$

÷ $\cos^2\theta$:   $\tan^2\theta + 1 \equiv \sec^2\theta$

÷ $\sin^2\theta$:   $1 + \cot^2\theta \equiv \operatorname{cosec}^2\theta$

📈 Graphs: asymptotes where the parent graph is 0 (Web Lab, Tab 1).

Sketch y = sec x

Meeting 1 · Elaborate & Evaluate

Worked Example

Solve $2\tan^2\theta + \sec\theta = 1$, $0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ$.

$2(\sec^2\theta - 1) + \sec\theta - 1 = 0 \Rightarrow 2\sec^2\theta + \sec\theta - 3 = 0$

$(2\sec\theta + 3)(\sec\theta - 1) = 0 \Rightarrow \cos\theta = -\frac23$ or $\cos\theta = 1$

$\theta = 0^\circ, 131.8^\circ, 228.2^\circ, 360^\circ$

Formulae

$\sin(A \pm B) \equiv \sin A\cos B \pm \cos A\sin B$

Meeting 3 · Explain Three Faces of cos 2A

Formulae

$\sin 2A \equiv 2\sin A\cos A$    $\tan 2A \equiv \dfrac{2\tan A}{1 - \tan^2 A}$

$\cos 2A \equiv \cos^2 A - \sin^2 A \equiv 2\cos^2 A - 1 \equiv 1 - 2\sin^2 A$

$\cos^2 A \equiv \frac{1 + \cos 2A}{2}$    $\sin^2 A \equiv \frac{1 - \cos 2A}{2}$

Card Sort Which form?

Other terms in $\sin x$ → $1 - 2\sin^2 x$ · in $\cos x$ → $2\cos^2 x - 1$ · with $\sin 2x$ → factorise, never divide!

Meeting 4 · Explain Proof Strategies

Toolbox

  1. Start from the more complicated side.
  2. Rewrite in $\sin$ and $\cos$.
  3. Use $\sin^2 + \cos^2 \equiv 1$, $1 + \tan^2 \equiv \sec^2$, $1 + \cot^2 \equiv \operatorname{cosec}^2$, double angles.
  4. Combine fractions; factorise ($a^2 - b^2$).
  5. Never move terms across ≡.

Equations: turn into a quadratic in one ratio, e.g. $\cot^2\theta = \operatorname{cosec}^2\theta - 1$.

Prove: (1 + cot²θ) sin²θ ≡ 1

Meeting 4 · Elaborate & Evaluate

Worked Example

Solve $3\cot^2\theta - 5\operatorname{cosec}\theta + 1 = 0$, $0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ$.

$3(\operatorname{cosec}^2\theta - 1) - 5\operatorname{cosec}\theta + 1 = 0 \Rightarrow 3\operatorname{cosec}^2\theta - 5\operatorname{cosec}\theta - 2 = 0$

$(3\operatorname{cosec}\theta + 1)(\operatorname{cosec}\theta - 2) = 0$; $\operatorname{cosec}\theta = -\frac13$ impossible

$\sin\theta = \frac12 \Rightarrow \theta = 30^\circ, 150^\circ$

Meeting 5 · Explain The R-Formula

Method $a\sin\theta + b\cos\theta \equiv R\sin(\theta + \alpha)$

$R\sin\theta\cos\alpha + R\cos\theta\sin\alpha$ → $R\cos\alpha = a$, $R\sin\alpha = b$

$R = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$,   $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{b}{a}$

Max $= R$ when $\theta + \alpha = 90^\circ$; min $= -R$ when $\theta + \alpha = 270^\circ$.

$3\sin\theta + 4\cos\theta \equiv 5\sin(\theta + 53.13^\circ)$

Minus sign? Use $R\sin(\theta - \alpha)$ or $R\cos(\theta \pm \alpha)$ so that $R > 0$, $\alpha$ acute.
